Clement of Alexandria called John "a spiritual Gospel," and the phrase has stuck because it is exact. Where Matthew, Mark, and Luke largely narrate the events of Jesus' ministry, John interprets them — slowing down, circling back, turning each scene over until its meaning shows. It is the same Jesus, told in a different key.

That key is set in the **prologue** (<VerseRef book="john" chapter={1} verse="1-18" />), one of the most theologically dense passages in the New Testament, which frames Jesus as the eternal Word — the *Logos* — through whom all things were made, now become flesh.

The Gospel then falls into two halves. The **book of signs** (chapters 2–12) records seven miracles — beginning with water turned to wine and culminating in the raising of Lazarus — each followed by discourse that unfolds what the sign reveals about Jesus' identity. The **book of glory** (chapters 13–21) turns inward and then upward: the farewell discourses to the disciples (chapters 13–17), the passion (chapters 18–19), and the resurrection appearances (chapters 20–21).

Running through it all are John's seven "I AM" sayings, each a self-revelation:

- "I am the bread of life" (<VerseRef book="john" chapter={6} verse={35} />)
- "I am the light of the world" (<VerseRef book="john" chapter={8} verse={12} />)
- "I am the door of the sheep" (<VerseRef book="john" chapter={10} verse={7} />)
- "I am the good shepherd" (<VerseRef book="john" chapter={10} verse={11} />)
- "I am the resurrection, and the life" (<VerseRef book="john" chapter={11} verse={25} />)
- "I am the way, the truth, and the life" (<VerseRef book="john" chapter={14} verse={6} />)
- "I am the true vine" (<VerseRef book="john" chapter={15} verse={1} />)

The Greek behind each — *egō eimi*, "I am" — deliberately echoes the name God speaks to Moses at the burning bush (<VerseRef book="exodus" chapter={3} verse={14} />).

John is also the only evangelist who pauses to tell us why he wrote: "these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name" (<VerseRef book="john" chapter={20} verse="30-31" />). He pursues that aim with a deliberately small vocabulary — *word*, *life*, *light*, *love*, *abide*, *glory* — each term used so often, and so deliberately, that by the Gospel's end it has accumulated a depth the Synoptics' wider vocabulary never attempts.
