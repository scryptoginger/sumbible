Judges 11 is the Jephthah cycle's central chapter — the cycle's call, negotiation, victory, and vow all narrated in a single chapter. Six movements compose the chapter: Jephthah's origin and exile (11:1-3); the elders of Gilead's recruitment and Jephthah's negotiation of terms (11:4-11); the king-of-Ammon diplomatic negotiation (11:12-28); the Spirit of the LORD + the vow + the victory (11:29-33); Jephthah's return + his daughter's coming out (11:34-38); the vow's execution + the annual commemoration (11:39-40).

**Jephthah's origin and exile** (11:1-3). The chapter opens at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={11} verse={1} />: "Now Jephthah the Gileadite was a mighty man of valour, and he was the son of an harlot: and Gilead begat Jephthah." The juxtaposition is the chapter's first signal — *gibbor chayil* (mighty man of valour) and *ben-ishah zonah* (son of a harlot) at the same verse, the same person. The half-brothers' rejection at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={11} verse={2} /> ("Thou shalt not inherit in our father's house; for thou art the son of a strange woman") drives Jephthah to the land of Tob, where "vain men" gather to him — the same Hebrew *reqim* used at Judg 9:4 for the men Abimelech hired with Baalberith silver (Session 26 SB1). The narrative registers Jephthah's pre-call status as similar-but-not-identical to Abimelech's: both gather *reqim*; Jephthah does not finance them with cult treasury; Jephthah does not seek headship until summoned.

**The elders of Gilead's recruitment + Jephthah's negotiation of terms** (11:4-11). Ammon's war with Israel makes the chapter's leadership-vacancy at Judg 10:18 acute; the elders of Gilead come to Tob (11:5). Jephthah's confrontation at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={11} verse={7} /> ("Did not ye hate me, and expel me out of my father's house? and why are ye come unto me now when ye are in distress?") refuses easy acceptance. The elders' answer at 11:8 elevates the offer from captain (11:6) to *rosh* (head) over all the inhabitants of Gilead. Jephthah secures the terms at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={11} verse={9} /> ("If ye bring me home again to fight against the children of Ammon, and the LORD deliver them before me, shall I be your head?"); the elders swear ("The LORD be witness between us"); Jephthah is made head and captain. The chapter at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={11} verse={11} /> closes the recruitment with a covenantal-frame notation: "Jephthah uttered all his words before the LORD in Mizpeh." The negotiation has been brought into liturgical space; what follows operates under the LORD's witness.

**The king-of-Ammon negotiation** (11:12-28). Jephthah's first move is diplomatic, not military. He sends messengers to the king of Ammon with a direct question at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={11} verse={12} />: "What hast thou to do with me, that thou art come against me to fight in my land?" Ammon's reply at 11:13 makes the territorial claim: Israel took Ammon's land "from Arnon even unto Jabbok, and unto Jordan" on the way out of Egypt; restore those lands peaceably. Jephthah's second-round response at 11:15-27 is the OT's most-developed pre-monarchic diplomatic historical-recap (see LangNote below).

Three structural elements distinguish the negotiation: (1) the legal-historical refutation — Israel took only Amorite land that the LORD God of Israel had given them (11:15-22), not Moabite or Ammonite land; (2) the Chemosh-comparative-religious-juridical argument at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={11} verse={24} />: "Wilt not thou possess that which Chemosh thy god giveth thee to possess? So whomsoever the LORD our God shall drive out from before us, them will we possess." The comparative-structure operates at the ANE-international-juridical register without affirming Chemosh's existence as such — the argument's force runs through the parallel-structure (each people takes what their god gives) rather than through theological endorsement of Chemosh as a real deity. (3) The three-hundred-years chronological-prescription argument at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={11} verse={26} /> — territorial claims unasserted for three centuries lack standing. The negotiation closes at 11:27 with the LORD-as-Judge formula: "the LORD the Judge be judge this day between the children of Israel and the children of Ammon." The king of Ammon does not hearken (11:28); the diplomatic phase ends.

**The Spirit of the LORD + the vow + the victory** (11:29-33). The Spirit-empowerment formula at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={11} verse={29} /> ("Then the Spirit of the LORD came upon Jephthah") joins the cycle's prior instances at Othniel (3:10), Gideon (6:34) — drafted Sessions 24 and 25. The vow at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={11} verse="30-31" /> follows: "And Jephthah vowed a vow unto the LORD, and said, If thou shalt without fail deliver the children of Ammon into mine hands, Then it shall be, that whatsoever cometh forth of the doors of my house to meet me, when I return in peace from the children of Ammon, shall surely be the LORD's, and I will offer it up for a burnt offering." Two textual features merit attention before the narrative continues. The Hebrew *<LangNote term="olah" script="עוֹלָה" language="Hebrew" gloss="‘Burnt offering’ — the sacrificial category in which the entire offering is consumed on the altar, distinct from peace-offerings (shelamim) or sin-offerings (chattat). Leviticus 1 details the olah's procedural requirements; the offering must be a male without blemish (ox, sheep, or goat) or a turtledove / young pigeon (Lev 1:3, 10, 14). The Judg 11:31 vow's specification of olah is the textual hinge that opens the literal-burnt-offering reading of the vow's interpretive spectrum at 11:39.">olah</LangNote>* (burnt offering) terminology at 11:31 is part of the textual hinge that opens the interpretive spectrum at 11:39 (see LangNote below). The Hebrew <LangNote term="vav-construction" script="וְ" language="Hebrew" gloss="‘And’ / ‘or’ — the Hebrew vav-conjunction is syntactically ambiguous and permits both coordinative ('and') and disjunctive ('or') readings depending on context. At Judg 11:31, the construction 'shall surely be the LORD's, and I will offer it up' (KJV) renders the vav coordinatively; some commentators (including significant rabbinic and some Christian readings) argue the vav can be read disjunctively as 'shall surely be the LORD's, or I will offer it up' — yielding a non-literal-sacrifice option (if a clean animal comes out, offer it as a burnt offering; if a person comes out, they shall be the LORD's in dedication). The syntactic ambiguity is the textual hinge of the interpretive spectrum reported at the vow's execution.">vav-construction</LangNote> at 11:31 — "and I will offer it up" / "or I will offer it up" — is the syntactic hinge between two principal readings of what the vow committed to. The narrative proceeds without resolving the ambiguity: Ammon is delivered into Jephthah's hand at 11:32; twenty cities are struck from Aroer to Minnith at 11:33; "the children of Ammon were subdued before the children of Israel."

**Jephthah's return + his daughter's coming out** (11:34-38). The chapter's pivot comes at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={11} verse={34} />: "And Jephthah came to Mizpeh unto his house, and, behold, his daughter came out to meet him with timbrels and with dances: and she was his only child; beside her he had neither son nor daughter." Jephthah's response at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={11} verse={35} /> is *rending of clothes* and grief — "Alas, my daughter! thou hast brought me very low, and thou art one of them that trouble me: for I have opened my mouth unto the LORD, and I cannot go back." The daughter's response at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={11} verse={36} /> is submission: "My father, if thou hast opened thy mouth unto the LORD, do to me according to that which hath proceeded out of thy mouth; forasmuch as the LORD hath taken vengeance for thee of thine enemies, even of the children of Ammon." Her request at 11:37 is for two months to "go up and down upon the mountains, and bewail my virginity, I and my fellows." Jephthah grants the request (11:38).

**The vow's execution + the annual commemoration** (11:39-40). The chapter closes at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={11} verse="39-40" />: "And it came to pass at the end of two months, that she returned unto her father, who did with her according to his vow which he had vowed: and she knew no man. And it was a custom in Israel, That the daughters of Israel went yearly to lament the daughter of Jephthah the Gileadite four days in a year." The execution-phrasing — *did with her according to his vow* + *she knew no man* — together with the annual four-day commemoration are the chapter's narrative-closure data; the interpretive spectrum that reads these data is examined in the LangNote below.

<LangNotes>

**Jephthah's vow + Jephthah's daughter at Judg 11:30-40 — four-position interpretive spectrum reported without arbitration.** The vow and its execution sit in one of the canonical narrative's most-discussed interpretive spectra. SumBible reports the four position-types per §0 Prime Directive #5 (when uncertain, omit arbitration) and §6.0 (spectrum-reporting on contested interpretations) — consistent with the Ehud / Jael / mal'akh-YHWH theophany / evil-spirit-from-God spectrum-handling at Sessions 24 + 25 + 26. The canonical narrative's own text-internal framing data is the firm ground; the four position-types read that data without canonical-arbitration:

**Text-internal data the positions interpret:** (a) the vow's *'olah* (burnt offering) terminology at 11:31; (b) the Hebrew vav-construction at 11:31 — "and I will offer" / "or I will offer" — whose syntactic ambiguity permits both coordinative and disjunctive readings; (c) the daughter's request to *bewail her virginity* (not her death) at 11:37; (d) the execution-phrasing at 11:39 — *did with her according to his vow* + *she knew no man*; (e) the annual four-day commemoration at 11:40.

(1) **Literal burnt-offering reading.** The vow's *'olah* terminology + the execution-phrasing "did with her according to his vow which he had vowed" read at the literal-sacrifice register. Substantial historic Christian and Jewish commentary holds this position. On this reading, Jephthah's vow committed him to a burnt-offering of whatever first came out of his house to meet him; the daughter came out; the execution at 11:39 carried out the vow as literally formulated. The reading takes the *'olah* terminology and the execution-phrasing as straightforward and reads the bewailing of virginity as the daughter's grief at dying without descendants — a particular form of grief in a culture where descendants composed legacy.

(2) **Lifelong-virginity-dedication reading.** The "bewail her virginity" (not her death) at 11:37 + "she knew no man" at 11:39 + the annual four-day commemoration at 11:40 read at the celibacy-dedication-to-the-LORD register. The Hebrew vav-construction at 11:31 permits the "or I will offer" non-literal-sacrifice reading on this position — *whatsoever cometh forth shall surely be the LORD's, or [if appropriate] I will offer it up for a burnt offering*. On the disjunctive reading, the vow's structure is: if a clean animal comes out, offer it as a burnt offering; if a person comes out, they shall be the LORD's (dedicated to lifelong service). The execution at 11:39 is the daughter's perpetual virginity rather than her sacrifice. Significant rabbinic commentary (notably medieval — Kimhi and others) and some Christian commentary hold this position. The daughter's bewailing-of-virginity rather than bewailing-of-death is the position's strongest internal textual support; the *'olah* terminology and the "did with her according to his vow" phrasing are read against the disjunctive vav.

(3) **The cross-corpus child-sacrifice prohibition framework operating as the chapter's broader canonical context.** Whatever the chapter at hand records, the Pentateuchal framework prohibits child-sacrifice to YHWH in unambiguous terms: Leviticus 18:21 — "And thou shalt not let any of thy seed pass through the fire to Molech, neither shalt thou profane the name of thy God: I am the LORD." Leviticus 20:2-5 — "Whosoever he be of the children of Israel, or of the strangers that sojourn in Israel, that giveth any of his seed unto Molech; he shall surely be put to death." Deuteronomy 12:31 — "every abomination to the LORD, which he hateth, have they done unto their gods; for even their sons and their daughters they have burnt in the fire to their gods." Deuteronomy 18:10 — "There shall not be found among you any one that maketh his son or his daughter to pass through the fire." The four anchors compose the canonical prohibition framework. The Judges narrative records Jephthah's vow and its execution *without commenting on the framework's operation*; the prohibition operates as the broader canonical context against which the chapter's events are read by readers operating with the whole canon in view. The cross-corpus framework's force is preserved without requiring the Judges narrative to do the prohibition's editorial work explicitly.

(4) **Sacred-narrative-recording-without-endorsing reading.** Parallel to the Ehud, Jael, and Abimelech sober-handling spectra (Sessions 24 + 26): the canonical canvas records the historical-political reality of Jephthah's vow and its execution without underwriting it as morally exemplary. The Judges narrative's recording-mode is the same recording-mode the book uses for the Succoth/Penuel accountability (Judg 8 Session 25), the Abimelech bloodguilt (Judg 9 Session 26), and the inter-tribal-shibboleth slaughter (Judg 12 next chapter): the historical-political-action is reported; the cyclic-pattern's deteriorative trajectory frames the broader interpretive context. The chapter at hand's 11:39 execution-phrasing carries no editorial-commentary; the chapter closes with the annual commemoration, which itself can be read as the canonical narrative's continuing-memorial-of-the-cost without arbitrating the vow's metaphysical status.

SumBible does not arbitrate among the four positions. The text-internal data is the firm ground; the four position-types operate at the interpretive level; the canonical narrative carries the spectrum forward without resolving it within Judges 11 itself. Same discipline Sessions 24 + 26 held on Ehud, Jael, and the evil-spirit-from-God (Judg 9:23, drafted Session 26) — text-internal framework foregrounded; interpretive positions reported; no arbitration.

**Hebrews 11:32 cluster's third-member handling — faith-register vs moral-evaluation-register distinction.** Jephthae is the third of the four Judges-figures named in Hebrews 11:32's faith-paradigm cloud-of-witnesses: "And what shall I more say? for the time would fail me to tell of Gedeon, and of Barak, and of Samson, and of Jephthae" (KJV, fetch-verified Session 24 SB3). The Hebrews-author's selection registers Jephthah at the faith-register — the act of negotiating-with-Ammon-and-fighting-under-the-Spirit-of-the-LORD that the Judg 11:29-33 narrative records — rather than at the moral-evaluation register that the vow-and-execution at 11:30-40 opens.

The register-distinction Session 25 SB2 Judg 8 LangNote framed for Gideon operates here at the same structural pattern: faith-register at Heb 11:32 vs moral-evaluation register at the chapter's moral-complication-event. For Gideon, the moral-complication was the ephod-compromise at Judg 8:24-27 ("which thing became a snare unto Gideon, and to his house"). For Jephthah, the moral-complication is the vow-and-its-execution at Judg 11:30-40. The Hebrews-author's selection-frame is the same in both cases: what the figure trusted in (the LORD's deliverance) and how the figure acted on that trust (the trumpets-and-torches assault for Gideon; the Spirit-empowered conquest of Ammon for Jephthah). The moral-evaluation register that the OT narrative opens is not the Hebrews-author's selection-frame. Two registers operate on the same person at different evaluation-axes; canonical narrative carries both as legitimate without resolution.

The Heb 11:34 general description ("out of weakness were made strong, waxed valiant in fight, turned to flight the armies of the aliens" — fetch-verified Session 24 SB3) fits the Jephthah cycle at multiple points: "out of weakness were made strong" fits the harlot-son-thrust-out-by-his-brothers origin at 11:1-3 + the Spirit-empowerment at 11:29; "waxed valiant in fight" fits the conquest of Ammon at 11:32-33; "turned to flight the armies of the aliens" fits Ammon's subduing at 11:33. The same description fit Gideon at Judg 7 (Session 25 SB2 LangNote); the Hebrews-author's general description applies to multiple cluster-members in different specific instantiations.

The Heb 11:32 cluster's broader pattern across all four figures (Gedeon + Barak + Samson + Jephthae) is the Hebrews-author's selection-discipline: figures whose Judges-narrative carries substantial moral complexity, read at the faith-register the canonical OT narrative itself does not always foreground. The reading is theological-typological at the NT-canonical-redaction level, not exegetical-arbitration of the OT-narrative's evaluation. Same register-distinction will operate on Samson — Heb 11:32 cluster's fourth member, future-session — where the faith-register vs moral-evaluation-register tension reaches its sharpest single-figure instance.
