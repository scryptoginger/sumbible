Judges 3 narrates three judge-cycles in sequence — the framework installed at Judges 2:11-23 (Sub-Batch 1 prior block) now begins narrative execution. Four movements: the remnant-nations preface (3:1-6); Othniel's compressed-clean first iteration (3:7-11); Ehud's elaborated subversive-narrative (3:12-30); Shamgar's one-verse minor-judge notation (3:31).

**The remnant-nations preface** (3:1-6). The chapter opens at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={3} verse={1} />: "Now these are the nations which the LORD left, to prove Israel by them, even as many of Israel as had not known all the wars of Canaan." The 2:22 framework-decision ("that through them I may prove Israel" — Sub-Batch 1 prior chapter) is now being applied to a specific generation: those who had not personally fought the Canaanite wars under Joshua. The chapter names the remnant-nations at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={3} verse={3} /> — "five lords of the Philistines, and all the Canaanites, and the Sidonians, and the Hivites" — and then records Israel's actual response at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={3} verse="5-6" />: "And the children of Israel dwelt among the Canaanites, Hittites, and Amorites, and Perizzites, and Hivites, and Jebusites: And they took their daughters to be their wives, and gave their daughters to their sons, and served their gods." The intermarriage-and-foreign-worship is the same pattern Joshua's farewell at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={23} verse="11-13" /> (Session 23 Sub-Batch 5) warned against; Israel's actual response confirms the warning's necessity.

**Othniel — the model-judge** (3:7-11). The Othniel cycle is the framework's reference-form. Every stage of the cyclic-pattern installed at Judges 2:11-23 is named explicitly: did evil and forgot the LORD (3:7); sold into the hand of Chushan-rishathaim king of Mesopotamia eight years (3:8); cried unto the LORD (3:9 first half); the LORD raised up a deliverer (3:9 second half); the Spirit of the LORD came upon him (3:10 first half); deliverance and victory (3:10 second half); land at rest forty years (3:11 first half); Othniel died (3:11 second half). The compressed-clean form deliberately establishes the framework's reference-pattern. Othniel is also — as the LangNote below details — the figure who closes the Joshua-to-Judges narrative bridge installed at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={1} verse="12-15" /> (Sub-Batch 1 prior block).

**Ehud — the subversive-narrative deliverer** (3:12-30). Ehud's cycle elaborates the framework with the book of Judges' longest assassination-scene. The cycle opens at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={3} verse={12} />: "And the children of Israel did evil again in the sight of the LORD: and the LORD strengthened Eglon the king of Moab against Israel." Eglon's eighteen-year oppression includes coalition with Ammon and Amalek and the capture of "the city of palm trees" (Jericho's region, 3:13). Israel cries; the LORD raises up Ehud at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={3} verse={15} />: "Ehud the son of Gera, a Benjamite, a man lefthanded." The Benjamite-irony — Ehud is left-handed, his tribal name Benjamin means "son of the right hand" — is foregrounded immediately (see LangNote below).

The assassination-scene at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={3} verse="16-23" /> includes: a concealed two-edged cubit-length dagger girt under Ehud's raiment on his right thigh (where right-handed guards would not search); the presentation of the tribute to Eglon "a very fat man" (3:17); the dismissal of the tribute-bearers; Ehud's return from "the quarries that were by Gilgal" with the report "I have a secret errand unto thee, O king" (3:19); the meeting in the summer parlour Eglon "had for himself alone" (3:20); Ehud's "I have a message from God unto thee" (3:20 — the religious-pretext ruse that the interpretive spectrum below registers); Eglon rises; Ehud thrusts the dagger into Eglon's belly with such force that "the haft also went in after the blade; and the fat closed upon the blade, so that he could not draw the dagger out of his belly; and the dirt came out" (3:22); Ehud escapes through the porch, locking the doors behind him. The servants' delayed discovery at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={3} verse={24} /> reads the locked doors as "Surely he covereth his feet in his summer chamber" — a euphemism for relieving himself; they "tarried till they were ashamed" before opening with a key (3:25). The scene's narrative-humor is unmistakable — the only judge-cycle in the book with sustained comic register.

The deliverance proper follows at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={3} verse="27-30" />: Ehud blows the trumpet in mount Ephraim; the Israelites take the fords of Jordan toward Moab; ten thousand Moabites are slain; "And the land had rest fourscore years" (3:30) — eighty years, double Othniel's forty.

**Shamgar — the first minor judge** (3:31). The chapter closes with the framework's first minor-judge notation at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={3} verse={31} />: "And after him was Shamgar the son of Anath, which slew of the Philistines six hundred men with an ox goad: and he also delivered Israel." No oppressor-naming, no cycle-completion, no rest-years counted. Shamgar's one-verse notation establishes the form-as-substance precedent for the five remaining minor judges (Tola, Jair, Ibzan, Elon, Abdon) whose notations will collectively occupy fewer verses than Samson's single chapter. The Philistines appear here for the first time as Israel's antagonist — the same enemy that will dominate the Samson narrative (Judg 13-16) and the early-Samuel narrative (1 Sam 4 onward).

**Othniel as model-judge — the Joshua-to-Judges bridge closes.** Othniel is the only figure in the canonical narrative to appear in three structurally-decisive cross-book positions: Joshua 15:16-19 (the Caleb-son-in-law Debir-capture micro-narrative), Judges 1:12-15 (the same narrative replayed verbatim at the Judges-opening — drafted Sub-Batch 1 this session), and Judges 3:7-11 (the cyclic-pattern's first iteration). The three appearances install a deliberate narrative-architectural bridge: the Joshua-period's last named individual-narrative is the Judges-period's first judge-cycle's protagonist. No other named figure performs this bridging-function across the two books at the depth Othniel does.

The Othniel cycle's narrative-function is reference-form. Every stage of the cyclic-pattern installed at Judges 2:11-23 is named in exactly one verse-segment, with no elaboration or variation. Subsequent cycles will compress particular stages (the cry to the LORD often omitted or referenced only briefly), elaborate particular stages (Ehud's assassination-scene; Gideon's fleece + 300-warriors selection; Samson's serial-failures), or substitute particular stages (Jephthah's bargain-with-elders replacing the LORD-raises-up-deliverer formula). Each variation is read against Othniel's reference-pattern. The 3:10 "And the Spirit of the LORD came upon him" is also the cycle's first instance of the Spirit-empowerment formula that becomes a recurring marker across the major-judge cycles — Gideon at 6:34, Jephthah at 11:29, Samson at 13:25 + 14:6 + 14:19 + 15:14.

**Ehud's subversive-narrative — the interpretive spectrum reported without arbitration.** The Ehud-Eglon scene is one of the canonical narrative's most-discussed morally-complex episodes. Three structural features carry the subversive register, and the interpretive spectrum responds to each:

(1) **The Benjamite-irony.** Ehud is left-handed; his tribe name Benjamin means "son of the right hand" (Gen 35:18 — Jacob's renaming of Benoni). The tribal-and-personal contradiction is foregrounded by 3:15's explicit double-naming ("a Benjamite, a man lefthanded"). The interpretive spectrum reads this either as deliberate divine-inversion (the LORD uses what the surrounding culture considered weakness or anomaly for deliverance — the cross-canon pattern recurring at 1 Sam 16:7 "the LORD looketh on the heart" and 1 Cor 1:27 "God hath chosen the foolish things of the world") or as biographical-detail noted without theological-weight.

(2) **The religious-pretext ruse.** Ehud's "I have a message from God unto thee" (3:20) is the assassination's opening ruse. The interpretive spectrum runs across positions: heroic-deliverance reading treats the ruse as legitimate-stratagem-in-warfare (the LORD-raised-up deliverer using available means; the moral evaluation belongs to the wartime context, not the deception per se); morally-problematic-deception reading treats the use of "message from God" as a deception-cost the narrative records without arbitrating; sacred-narrative-recording-without-endorsing reading treats the canonical canvas as recording the historical-and-political reality of the deliverance without underwriting every operational detail as morally exemplary. The same spectrum operates on the Jael narrative at Judg 4:21 (next chapter), with the spectrum's structural-form there closely parallel.

(3) **The narrative humor.** The "covereth his feet" euphemism at 3:24, the servants' embarrassed delay, the "very fat man" detail at 3:17, the "dirt came out" at 3:22 (Heb. *parshedonah*, an uncertain word — possibly referring to bodily emission or possibly to an architectural feature of the parlour) all carry sustained comic register. The Ehud cycle is the only judge-cycle in Judges with significant narrative humor. The comic register is itself a distinctive form-feature of this cycle; subsequent cycles return to the sober-cyclical-narrative register that governs the rest of the book.

SumBible's handling per §0 Prime Directive #5 (when uncertain, omit) and §6.0 (spectrum-reporting on contested interpretations without arbitration): the spectrum is named, the structural-features are identified, the multi-position interpretive responses are noted, and no single reading is advocated as canonical-correct.

**Form-as-substance: three cycles in one chapter; cycle-length doubling.** The chapter's structural-form mirrors its theological-content. Three cycles in sequence: Othniel's 11 verses including 40-year rest; Ehud's 19 verses including 80-year rest (double); Shamgar's 1 verse with no rest-years counted. The cycle-length doubling from Othniel to Ehud (40 → 80) is a notable feature — the longest period of rest in the book — and it stands in narrative-tension with the cyclic-decline trajectory installed at Judges 2:19 ("more than their fathers"). The framework's deteriorative trajectory operates at the apostasy-depth level (each cycle's apostasy is worse), not necessarily at the rest-duration level (each cycle's rest may be shorter or longer depending on the deliverer's narrative). Subsequent cycles will show rest-period variation: Deborah's 40 years (Judg 5:31); Gideon's 40 years (Judg 8:28); Jephthah's 6 years (Judg 12:7); Samson's 20 years (Judg 15:20, with no rest-statement after Samson's death). The doubling here at Ehud's 80 years is the framework's high-water-mark.
