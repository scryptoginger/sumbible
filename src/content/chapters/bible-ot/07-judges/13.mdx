Judges 13 opens the Samson cycle — the cyclic-pattern's final major-judge instance before the appendix-narratives. Five movements compose the chapter: the Philistine oppression's onset (13:1); the angel's first appearance to Manoah's wife (13:2-7); the angel's second appearance to Manoah and his wife together (13:8-18); the offering scene + the ascending-in-the-flame + the recognition (13:19-23); Samson's birth + the Spirit of the LORD's first moving (13:24-25).

**The Philistine oppression** (13:1). The chapter opens at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={13} verse={1} />: "And the children of Israel did evil again in the sight of the LORD; and the LORD delivered them into the hand of the Philistines forty years." The forty-year oppression is the longest single-cycle oppression notation in the book of Judges. The chapter records no cry-unto-the-LORD at the cycle's opening; the cyclic-pattern's distress → cry → judge-raised sequence is compressed or absent. The LORD acts at the announcement stage rather than at the cry stage — the Samson cycle's distinctive structural feature.

**The angel's first appearance to Manoah's wife** (13:2-7). The chapter introduces Manoah of Zorah, of the family of the Danites, whose wife is barren. The angel of the LORD appears to the woman at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={13} verse={3} /> with the standard barren-wife-and-promised-son announcement-form recurring across the canonical narrative (Sarah at Gen 18; Rachel at Gen 30; Hannah at 1 Sam 1). The announcement includes Nazirite-vow restrictions on both the mother during pregnancy and on the child perpetually at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={13} verse="4-5" />: "Now therefore beware, I pray thee, and drink not wine nor strong drink, and eat not any unclean thing: For, lo, thou shalt conceive, and bear a son; and no razor shall come on his head: for the child shall be a <LangNote term="nazir" script="נָזִיר" language="Hebrew" gloss="‘Consecrated one’ / ‘set apart one,’ from the root nzr (to separate / consecrate). The Num 6 vow is voluntary and temporary; the Judg 13:5 Samson instance installs a perpetual-and-prenatal variant operating 'from the womb to the day of his death' (13:7). The principal cross-corpus parallels: Samuel (1 Sam 1:11 mother's vow); LXX-Christian-reception of John the Baptist (Luke 1:15). The "begin to deliver" notation at 13:5 is a distinctive structural element — Samson does not complete the deliverance; he begins it. The Philistine oppression continues into the early monarchy (1 Sam 4-7 and onward).

Manoah's wife reports the encounter at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={13} verse="6-7" />: "A man of God came unto me, and his countenance was like the countenance of an angel of God, very terrible: but I asked him not whence he was, neither told he me his name." The narrative-language at 13:6 — "a man of God" + "the countenance of an angel of God" + "very terrible" — registers the perception-and-identification ambiguity that the four-position spectrum reads (see LangNote below).

**The angel's second appearance to Manoah and his wife** (13:8-18). Manoah prays at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={13} verse={8} /> for the man of God to return; "And God hearkened to the voice of Manoah." The second appearance comes to Manoah's wife again, alone in the field; she runs to fetch Manoah; Manoah comes to the man and asks for instruction about "the child that shall be born" (13:12). The angel's response at 13:13-14 restates the Nazirite restrictions on the mother. Then comes the chapter's most-discussed exchange at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={13} verse="15-18" />:

Manoah offers a meal (13:15); the angel refuses to eat and redirects to a burnt offering "unto the LORD" (13:16), with the narrative parenthetical "For Manoah knew not that he was an angel of the LORD" — registering Manoah's perception-and-identification ambiguity. Manoah asks for the angel's name at 13:17 ("that when thy sayings come to pass we may do thee honour"); the angel's reply at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={13} verse={18} /> closes the exchange: "Why askest thou thus after my name, seeing it is <LangNote term="peli" script="פֶּלִי" language="Hebrew" gloss="‘Wonderful’ / ‘secret’ / ‘hidden’ — from the root pl' (to be wonderful / to be hidden / to be extraordinary). **The offering + the ascending-in-the-flame + the recognition** (13:19-23). Manoah offers the kid + meat-offering on the rock at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={13} verse={19} />: "And the angel did wonderously; and Manoah and his wife looked on." The angel's ascending in the flame at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={13} verse={20} /> ("when the flame went up toward heaven from off the altar, that the angel of the LORD ascended in the flame of the altar. And Manoah and his wife looked on it, and fell on their faces to the ground") is the trajectory's distinctive new element (see LangNote below). Manoah's recognition at 13:21 — "Then Manoah knew that he was an angel of the LORD" — installs the perception's resolution; Manoah's fear at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={13} verse={22} /> registers the holy-fear stage: "We shall surely die, because we have seen God." His wife's theological-logical reply at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={13} verse={23} /> closes the recognition scene: "If the LORD were pleased to kill us, he would not have received a burnt offering and a meat offering at our hands, neither would he have shewed us all these things, nor would as at this time have told us such things as these." The wife's argument operates from acceptance-of-the-offering as warrant of continued life — the canonical narrative's text-internal reasoning about how to interpret divine encounter.

**Samson's birth + the Spirit's first moving** (13:24-25). The cycle's birth-notation at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={13} verse={24} /> closes the announcement-and-fulfillment opening — "And the woman bare a son, and called his name Samson: and the child grew, and the LORD blessed him." The Spirit-empowerment formula's distinctive first instance at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={13} verse={25} /> closes the chapter: "And the Spirit of the LORD began to move him at times in the camp of Dan between Zorah and Eshtaol." The "began to move... at times" registers the Samson Spirit-empowerment as episodic-and-intermittent rather than continuous; the cycle's subsequent narrative (Judg 14-16) will record the specific Spirit-coming instances at 14:6, 14:19, 15:14, and 16:28 — each at a deliverance-act moment.

**The angel of the LORD theophany at Judg 13 — the four-instance theophany-commissioning trajectory completes.** The chapter is the fourth and final instance of the canonical OT theophany-commissioning trajectory anticipated at Session 25 Sub-Batch 1 (Judg 6 LangNote). The four instances together compose one of the OT's most-architected cross-corpus structural frameworks:

| Instance | Location | Commissioning |
|---|---|---|
| Exod 3:1-15 | Burning bush at Horeb | Moses commissioned for the Exodus |
| Josh 5:13-15 | Captain-of-the-LORD's-host at Jericho | Joshua commissioned for the conquest |
| Judg 6:11-24 | Angel under the oak at Ophrah | Gideon commissioned for Midian-deliverance |
| Judg 13 | Angel at Zorah | Samson announced-in-utero for Philistine-deliverance |

The four instances share recurring structural features: appearance to a reluctant or unprepared figure; commissioning or announcement; recognition-of-the-divine + holy-fear at perceiving the LORD's presence. The Judg 13 instance contributes three distinctive elements to the trajectory:

(1) **The announcement-in-utero variant.** Where the prior three instances commission an adult figure (Moses at Horeb, Joshua at Jericho, Gideon at Ophrah), Judg 13 announces the future deliverer before conception. The commissioning's primary interlocutor is Manoah's wife — twice the angel comes to her — rather than the future-deliverer himself. The variant registers a different structural mode: the deliverer's identity is set from the womb under perpetual Nazirite consecration; the commissioning-narrative operates on the parent generation rather than on the deliverer himself.

(2) **The ascending-in-the-flame motif at 13:20.** Distinct from the Exod 3 burning-bush (the bush burns without being consumed), the Josh 5 sword-bearer (the captain stands with drawn sword), and the Judg 6 fire-rising-from-the-rock (the fire consumes the offering and the angel departs without ascending-motion), the Judg 13 instance has the angel ascending *in* the flame — the offering-flame becomes the vehicle of the angel's departure. The motif's structural-significance: the offering Manoah brings unwittingly to the angel-of-the-LORD is received-and-used as the means of the theophany's closure. The wife's theological-logical reasoning at 13:23 ("If the LORD were pleased to kill us, he would not have received a burnt offering and a meat offering at our hands") operates on the acceptance-of-the-offering as warrant; the canonical narrative records both the angel's redirection-to-the-LORD at 13:16 and the wife's reasoning at 13:23 — together they register the theophany's text-internal interpretive framework.

(3) **The wonderful-name reply at 13:18.** The Hebrew *peli* (KJV: "secret"; Heb. root pl' shares with Isa 9:6's *pele* rendered "Wonderful") adds intentional-divine-name-reticence to the trajectory. The same root underlies the wrestling-figure's name-refusal at Gen 32:29 ("Wherefore is it that thou dost ask after my name?"); the canonical narrative carries the divine-name-reticence as a recurring theophany-feature. See vLog #3 for the Isa 9:6 form-resonance reception-history reading.

The same four-position mal'akh-YHWH spectrum reported at Session 25 SB1 Judg 6 LangNote — Christophany / angelophany-divine-messenger / commander-of-heavenly-host / Jewish-tradition angelic-messenger — applies here and is reported without arbitration per §0 Prime Directive #5. The chapter at hand's distinctive text-internal data (the announcement-in-utero variant; the ascending-in-the-flame motif; the wonderful-name reply) adds to the spectrum without resolving it. The trajectory's completion across all four instances preserves both registers (the angel-distinct-from-the-LORD register and the LORD-himself register) simultaneously; the canonical narrative carries the spectrum forward into Judges 14-16 (the Samson narrative) and into the NT-reception literature without arbitration.

**The Nazirite-vow's perpetual-and-prenatal variant — Samson, Samuel, and the John-the-Baptist NT-reception trajectory.** The standard Nazirite-vow framework installed at Num 6:1-21 (drafted prior project session) is voluntary and temporary — a lay-consecration the worshipper undertakes for a defined period and ends with a closing ceremony (Num 6:13-21). The Judg 13:5, 7 announcement installs a distinct variant: perpetual ("from the womb to the day of his death" — 13:7) and prenatal (the consecration begins before Samson is conceived).

Three principal cross-corpus parallels operate at the perpetual-and-prenatal variant register:

(1) **Samuel** at 1 Sam 1:11 — Hannah's vow that "if thou wilt indeed look on the affliction of thine handmaid... then I will give him unto the LORD all the days of his life, and there shall no razor come upon his head." The Samuel parallel installs the same perpetual + maternal-vow + no-razor framework at the cycle that immediately follows the appendix-narratives.

(2) **John the Baptist** at Luke 1:15 (NT-cross-corpus) — Gabriel's announcement to Zacharias that "he shall be great in the sight of the Lord, and shall drink neither wine nor strong drink; and he shall be filled with the Holy Ghost, even from his mother's womb." The Lucan announcement installs the same perpetual + prenatal + wine-prohibition framework, with the holy-spirit-from-the-womb element added. The Judg 13 / Luke 1 form-resonance is one of the OT-NT canon's clearest single canonical-form parallels at the announcement-narrative register; SumBible reports the form-resonance without claiming direct literary dependence.

(3) **Samson's narrative-violations of the perpetual vow.** The Samson cycle's recurring Nazirite-violations operate against the perpetual-vow framework the chapter at hand installs: corpse-contact at the lion's carcass honey (14:8-9, Session 28 SB2); the seven-day wedding feast (14:10, possible wine-contact); corpse-contact at the jawbone slaughter (15:15); the seven-locks revelation and shaving at 16:17 and 16:19 — the perpetual no-razor element's explicit violation. The vow-violation thread runs across Judg 14-16 in parallel with the Spirit-empowerment thread; the canonical narrative carries both threads without arbitrating the divine-instrumental-causation question (the parallel that operates at Judg 14:4's "his father and his mother knew not that it was of the LORD, that he sought an occasion against the Philistines" — Session 28 SB2 will hold this in mind).
