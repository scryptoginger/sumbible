Judges 14 narrates the Samson cycle's opening adult-action sequence: the Timnathite-wife pursuit, the lion encounter, the wedding feast and riddle, and the Spirit-empowered slaying at Ashkelon. Five movements compose the chapter: the pursuit (14:1-4); the lion + the honey (14:5-9); the riddle (14:10-15); the disclosure (14:16-18); the Ashkelon slaughter + the wife given to companion (14:19-20).

**Samson's pursuit + the narrator's parenthetical** (14:1-4). The chapter opens at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={14} verse={1} />: "And Samson went down to Timnath, and saw a woman in Timnath of the daughters of the Philistines." His parents' protest at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={14} verse={3} /> ("Is there never a woman among the daughters of thy brethren, or among all my people, that thou goest to take a wife of the uncircumcised Philistines?") invokes the canonical-marriage-discipline anchor (Deut 7:3-4's prohibition of intermarriage with the seven nations operates on the surface-narrative register here). Samson's reply at 14:3 is brief: "Get her for me; for she pleaseth me well."

The narrator's parenthetical at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={14} verse={4} /> shifts the interpretive frame: "But his father and his mother knew not that it was of the LORD, that he sought an occasion against the Philistines: for at that time the Philistines had dominion over Israel." The parenthetical operates as text-internal theological-explanation — the surface-narrative pursuit is framed by the narrator as the LORD's instrumental use of Samson's action for the Philistine-deliverance purpose. The believer-spectrum reads the parenthetical without canonical-arbitration (see LangNote below).

**The lion + the honey** (14:5-9). On the way down at the vineyards of Timnath, the lion encounter comes at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={14} verse="5-6" />: "behold, a young lion roared against him. And the Spirit of the LORD came mightily upon him, and he rent him as he would have rent a kid, and he had nothing in his hand: but he told not his father or his mother what he had done." The Spirit-empowerment formula recurs from the Othniel cycle (Judg 3:10) + Gideon (6:34) + Jephthah (11:29) — the deliverer cycles' shared installation-form. Samson's silence about the deed at 14:6 sets up the riddle's later mystery for the wedding companions.

On the later return to take the woman, Samson "turned aside to see the carcase of the lion: and, behold, there was a swarm of bees and honey in the carcase of the lion" (14:8). The taking-and-eating at 14:9 — Samson eats from the honey, gives it to his parents, "but he told not them that he had taken the honey out of the carcase of the lion" — is the cycle's first Nazirite-vow violation at the corpse-contact element (Num 6:6 prohibits the Nazirite from coming "near a dead body"). The chapter records the violation without narrator-commentary; the canonical narrative carries the Spirit-empowerment from 14:6 and the vow-violation at 14:8-9 in parallel without single-axis collapse (see LangNote below).

**The wedding feast + the riddle** (14:10-15). Samson's father goes down to the woman; Samson makes a feast (Heb. *mishteh*, a drinking-occasion at the standard form — see vLog #3 for the wine-prohibition tension this register opens). Thirty companions are brought to be with him at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={14} verse={11} />. The riddle Samson puts forth at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={14} verse={14} /> — "Out of the eater came forth meat, and out of the strong came forth sweetness" — operates as a wager-contest with thirty sheets and thirty changes of garments at stake. The three-day failure to expound is followed by the threat at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={14} verse={15} />: "Entice thy husband, that he may declare unto us the riddle, lest we burn thee and thy father's house with fire." The threat sets up Judg 15:6's Philistine-retaliation execution of the same threat — the chapter's text-internal causal-mechanism that the canonical narrative does not editorialize.

**The disclosure + the riddle's answer** (14:16-18). The wife's persistent weeping over the seven days extracts the answer; she tells the children of her people; the companions answer at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={14} verse={18} /> just before sundown on the seventh day: "What is sweeter than honey? And what is stronger than a lion?" Samson's reply at 14:18 — "If ye had not plowed with my heifer, ye had not found out my riddle" — closes the wager-contest with both the answer-recognition and the wife-betrayal-acknowledgment in a single phrase.

**The Ashkelon slaughter + the wife given to companion** (14:19-20). The Spirit-empowerment formula recurs at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={14} verse={19} />: "And the Spirit of the LORD came upon him, and he went down to Ashkelon, and slew thirty men of them, and took their spoil, and gave change of garments unto them which expounded the riddle. And his anger was kindled, and he went up to his father's house." The Spirit-empowerment is paired with anger-kindled in the same verse; the Spirit-and-anger juxtaposition is one of the chapter's distinctive structural-paradoxes — the canonical narrative records the divine-empowerment and the human-anger without explaining their relationship. The chapter closes at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={14} verse={20} />: "But Samson's wife was given to his companion, whom he had used as his friend." The framework is now set for Judg 15's vengeance cycle.

**The 14:4 "it was of the LORD" parenthetical + the divine-instrumental-causation register reported without arbitration.** The narrator's parenthetical at 14:4 is one of the OT's clearest single instances of explicit divine-instrumental-causation framing within a sober-handling narrative. The framing has been read across three position-types in the believer-spectrum, consistent with the Session 26 Sub-Batch 1 evil-spirit-from-God spectrum-handling discipline (Judg 9:23):

(1) **Divine permission framework.** The LORD permits Samson's pursuit and uses its consequences for the Philistine-deliverance purpose. The framework preserves a sharp distinction between God's permissive will and God's directive will — Samson's pursuit-of-the-Philistine-woman remains a moral-discipline-question that the parents' objection at 14:3 raises (per Deut 7:3-4's prohibition framework), while the divine-providence operates over the consequences. Some Christian commentary holds this position.

(2) **Divine instrumental causation.** The LORD actively prompts the pursuit as the means of the deliverance. The narrator's "it was of the LORD" reads as direct-causation language; Samson's pursuit operates as the LORD's instrument from the start. The framework matches the text's surface evaluation most tightly and reads the parenthetical at the strong-sovereignty register; some Christian commentary holds this position.

(3) **ANE moral-causation framework.** The Ancient Near Eastern worldview did not draw the modern primary-cause / secondary-cause distinction that systematic theology operates with. In ANE moral-causation, the high god is the ultimate source of all causation; the text reflects this without modern philosophical distinction. The framework reads the 14:4 phrasing as the canonical narrative's natural mode-of-expression rather than as a doctrinal-statement requiring resolution.

SumBible reports the spectrum without arbitration per §0 Prime Directive #5; the narrator's "it was of the LORD" parenthetical is the firm ground; the believer-spectrum's three positions operate at the interpretive level. The same parenthetical-divine-causation register recurs at 14:19 (Spirit-empowerment for the Ashkelon slaughter), 15:14 (Spirit-empowerment for the jawbone slaughter), and 16:28 (the strengthening-prayer at the Philistine-temple closure); the cycle's broader divine-instrumental-causation thread runs across all three SBs of this session without canonical-arbitration.

**Spirit-empowerment + Nazirite-violation parallel registers across the Samson cycle.** Two threads run in parallel across Judges 14-16 without single-axis collapse. The chapter at hand installs both threads' first instances:

- **Spirit-empowerment thread.** 14:6 (lion-rending); 14:19 (Ashkelon slaughter); recurring at 15:14 (jawbone) and 16:28 (Philistine-temple-strengthening-prayer). The formula "Spirit of the LORD came mightily" / "Spirit of the LORD came upon him" reads as the cycle's deliverance-empowerment-mechanism, consistent with the prior major-judges' Spirit-empowerment instances at Othniel (3:10), Gideon (6:34), and Jephthah (11:29).
- **Nazirite-violation thread.** 14:8-9 (corpse-contact at the lion's carcass honey — Num 6:6 prohibits the Nazirite from coming near a dead body); 14:10 (the wedding *mishteh* — the standard feast-form implies a drinking-occasion, and the Num 6:3-4 no-wine restriction operates against it); recurring at 15:15 (corpse-contact at the jawbone slaughter); 16:17 (the perpetual no-razor element's explicit revelation and 16:19's shaving — the chapter's most explicit vow-loss).

The two threads run in parallel without single-axis collapse — the chapter records the Spirit-empowerments and the vow-violations as text-internal data; the canonical narrative carries both registers as legitimate without arbitrating the Spirit-empowerment vs vow-violation tension. The same parallel-register discipline operates at the OT-narrative's other Spirit-empowerment + moral-complexity pairings (Saul at 1 Sam 10:6, 16:14 — the Spirit's coming and the Spirit's departing as a parallel framework); the canonical narrative carries the parallel without resolving the philosophical-theological question of how divine-empowerment and human-moral-action interact. Session 25 SB2 Judg 8 LangNote (Gideon faith-register vs ephod-compromise moral-evaluation-register) framed the same register-distinction; the Samson cycle's instance is the framework's most-developed cross-corpus pairing.
