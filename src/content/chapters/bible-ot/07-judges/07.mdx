Judges 7 narrates the deliverance proper — the 300 selection, the Midianite-dream confirmation, and the trumpets-and-torches victory. Four movements structure the chapter: the 300 selection (7:1-8); the Midianite-dream confirmation (7:9-15); the trumpets-and-torches victory (7:16-22); the pursuit and capture of Oreb and Zeeb (7:23-25).

**The 300 selection** (7:1-8). The chapter opens at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={7} verse={1} />: "Then Jerubbaal, who is Gideon, and all the people that were with him, rose up early, and pitched beside the well of Harod: so that the host of the Midianites were on the north side of them, by the hill of Moreh, in the valley." The framework-statement comes at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={7} verse={2} />: "The people that are with thee are too many for me to give the Midianites into their hands, lest Israel vaunt themselves against me, saying, Mine own hand hath saved me." The "Mine own hand hath saved me" framework is the cycle's distinctive theological contribution to the canonical narrative's deliverance-pattern (see LangNote below); it parallels Deut 8:17-18's standing warning against "My power and the might of mine hand hath gotten me this wealth" at the deliverance-from-Egypt and the entry-into-the-land registers.

The first reduction comes at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={7} verse={3} />: "Whosoever is fearful and afraid, let him return and depart early from mount Gilead. And there returned of the people twenty and two thousand; and there remained ten thousand." The fearful-sent-home procedure honors the Deut 20:8 standing instruction for Israel's wars ("the officers shall speak further unto the people, and they shall say, What man is there that is fearful and fainthearted? let him go and return unto his house, lest his brethren's heart faint as well as his heart"). The second reduction comes at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={7} verse="5-6" /> via the water-drinking test: "Every one that lappeth of the water with his tongue, as a dog lappeth, him shalt thou set by himself; likewise every one that boweth down upon his knees to drink. And the number of them that lapped, putting their hand to their mouth, were three hundred men." The interpretive-tradition history of the water-drinking criterion is reported without arbitration in vLog #3 — the canonical narrative records the procedure without explicitly theologizing the criterion. The LORD's commission at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={7} verse={7} /> closes the selection: "By the three hundred men that lapped will I save you, and deliver the Midianites into thine hand."

**The Midianite-dream confirmation** (7:9-15). The LORD's pastoral-condescension to Gideon's residual uncertainty operates at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={7} verse="10-11" />: "But if thou fear to go down, go thou with Phurah thy servant down to the host: And thou shalt hear what they say; and afterward shall thine hands be strengthened." Gideon and his servant descend to the camp's outskirts; the dream-and-interpretation comes at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={7} verse="13-14" />: "Behold, I dreamed a dream, and, lo, a cake of barley bread tumbled into the host of Midian, and came unto a tent, and smote it that it fell, and overturned it... This is nothing else save the sword of Gideon the son of Joash, a man of Israel: for into his hand hath God delivered Midian, and all the host." Gideon's response at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={7} verse={15} /> is worship: "when Gideon heard the telling of the dream, and the interpretation thereof, that he worshipped, and returned into the host of Israel."

**The trumpets-and-torches victory** (7:16-22). Gideon divides the 300 into three companies of 100 each, with a trumpet, an empty pitcher, and a lamp inside each pitcher for every man (7:16). The instructions at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={7} verse="17-18" /> include the coordinated trumpet-blowing and the cry; the execution at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={7} verse={20} /> registers the climactic cry: "The sword of the LORD, and of Gideon." The cry is repeated by all three companies in coordinated assault around the Midianite camp at the start of the middle watch.

The LORD's action at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={7} verse={22} /> closes the victory: "And the three hundred blew the trumpets, and the LORD set every man's sword against his fellow, even throughout all the host." The Midianite-internal slaughter pattern parallels the cosmic-warfare register of Josh 10:11 (the great stones from heaven at Beth-horon, Session 22) and Judg 5:20 (the stars in their courses fighting against Sisera, Session 24) — the LORD's deliverance operates through means that the 300 themselves did not enact. The "Mine own hand hath saved me" foreclosure of 7:2 is now operationalized as historical-reality: the 300 stood at their posts blowing trumpets and holding lamps; the LORD set the Midianite swords against their fellow Midianites.

**The pursuit** (7:23-25). The men of Naphtali, Asher, and "all Manasseh" gather to pursue (7:23); Gideon sends messengers throughout mount Ephraim, who take the fords of Jordan toward Bethbarah (7:24). The princes Oreb and Zeeb are captured at the rock and the winepress that subsequently take their names; Oreb's and Zeeb's heads are brought to Gideon "on the other side Jordan" (7:25), setting up the pursuit-narrative continuation at Judges 8.

<LangNotes>

**The "Mine own hand hath saved me" framework + the LORD-fights-for-Israel formula's most-developed instance.** The framework-statement at Judg 7:2 is the cycle's distinctive contribution to the OT-narrative's pattern of divine-deliverance framing. Three structural elements compose the framework:

(1) **The reduction's numerical-extremity at the form-as-substance register.** The reduction from 22,000 to 10,000 (after the fearful are sent home) to 300 (after the water-drinking test) is a 73-fold reduction. The numerical-comprehensive-significance of the reduction IS the theological substance: the disparity between the 300-man force and the 135,000-man Midianite-Amalekite-Easterner host (calculated from Judg 8:10's notation that 120,000 had fallen with 15,000 remaining) forecloses the "Mine own hand hath saved me" attribution at the actual-historical register. The cycle's framework operationalizes itself: it is not merely a doctrinal statement but a historical-procedural arrangement.

(2) **The Deuteronomic-anchor at Deut 8:17-18.** The Deut 8:17-18 standing warning ("And thou say in thine heart, My power and the might of mine hand hath gotten me this wealth. But thou shalt remember the LORD thy God: for it is he that giveth thee power to get wealth") installs the broader covenantal-framework that the Judg 7:2 framework-statement applies to the deliverance-from-oppression register. The Deuteronomic warning is general (all Israel's prosperity is in view); the Judges 7 application is specific (this particular deliverance is in view). The two operate at different scope-registers within the same theological framework.

(3) **The LORD-fights-for-Israel formula's most-developed instance in the canonical narrative.** The formula installed at Exod 14:14 ("The LORD shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace") and recurring across Joshua at 10:14 ("for the LORD fought for Israel") and 23:10 ("One man of you shall chase a thousand: for the LORD your God, he it is that fighteth for you") reaches its most-developed narrative-execution at Judg 7. The 300 + trumpets + pitchers + lamps + the LORD's setting every man's sword against his fellow together compose a deliverance-architecture in which the human-agency is structurally insufficient on its face. The Judges 7:22 LORD-action is the formula's clearest single instance: the 300 blew the trumpets, and the LORD set every Midianite's sword against his fellow. The human-agents stood and blew; the LORD enacted the deliverance.

The framework's NT reception is concentrated at <VerseRef book="hebrews" chapter={11} verse={32} /> + 11:34 — Gideon is the first of the four Judges-figures named in the faith-paradigm cloud-of-witnesses; the Heb 11:34 general description ("turned to flight the armies of the aliens") fits the Judg 7 deliverance precisely. The Heb 11:32 cluster handling will be fully developed at Judg 8 (next chapter), where the faith-paradigm reading sits in productive theological tension with the ephod-compromise reality the next chapter narrates.

The "sword of the LORD, and of Gideon" formula at 7:18, 20 is the LORD-fights-for-Israel framework's distinctive Judges-cycle articulation — the LORD and the human-deliverer named together in the battle-cry, with the LORD's name first and the human-deliverer's name second. The articulation registers the dual-agency the framework operationalizes: the LORD is the primary agent; the human-deliverer is the LORD's instrument; both are named together because both are operative.

</LangNotes>
