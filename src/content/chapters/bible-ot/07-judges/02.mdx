Judges 2 installs the book's interpretive framework. Three movements: the Bochim covenantal-lawsuit delivered by an angel of the LORD (2:1-5); the Joshua-death recall + the next-generation-knew-not-the-LORD notation (2:6-10); the cyclic-pattern's programmatic statement at 2:11-23 — the framework that governs the rest of the book.

**The Bochim covenantal-lawsuit** (2:1-5). The chapter opens at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={2} verse={1} />: "And an angel of the LORD came up from Gilgal to Bochim, and said, I made you to go up out of Egypt, and have brought you unto the land which I sware unto your fathers; and I said, I will never break my covenant with you." The opening lays the LORD's three foundational covenantal-acts as ground for the lawsuit that follows: the Exodus, the land-bringing, and the never-broken-covenant promise. The accusation comes at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={2} verse={2} />: "And ye shall make no league with the inhabitants of this land; ye shall throw down their altars: but ye have not obeyed my voice: why have ye done this?" The LORD's stipulation (no league + altar-destruction) traces back to Exod 23:32-33 + Deut 7:2-5 + the same charge Joshua delivered at his farewell.

The judgment-pronouncement comes at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={2} verse={3} />: "Wherefore I also said, I will not drive them out from before you; but they shall be as thorns in your sides, and their gods shall be a snare unto you." This is the explicit narrative-execution of Joshua's farewell-warning at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={23} verse={13} /> (drafted Session 23 Sub-Batch 5 — see LangNote below). The people respond at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={2} verse="4-5" /> with weeping and sacrifice: "the people lifted up their voice, and wept. And they called the name of that place Bochim: and they sacrificed there unto the LORD." Bochim (Heb. *bochim*, "weepers") marks the place by the people's response, not by the LORD's words.

**Joshua's death recalled + the next-generation notation** (2:6-10). The chapter then recapitulates Joshua's death at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={2} verse="6-9" /> in a form that overlaps with Joshua 24:29-31 (drafted Session 23 Sub-Batch 6) — the Judges-version functioning as the looking-back from the cyclic-pattern's installation-moment toward the closing of the Joshua period. The two versions share the people-serving-the-LORD-during-Joshua's-and-the-elders'-days notation; both record Joshua's death at 110 years; both record his burial in mount Ephraim. The two versions register one place-name variation: Joshua 24:30 reads "Timnath-serah" while Judges 2:9 reads "Timnath-heres" — the same place with the consonants of the second element reversed (s-r-ḥ vs ḥ-r-s; standard commentary explanation: scribal-tradition variation, possibly with rabbinic interpretive significance, but the same geographic location is in view).

The structurally-decisive notation comes at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={2} verse={10} />: "And also all that generation were gathered unto their fathers: and there arose another generation after them, which knew not the LORD, nor yet the works which he had done for Israel." This is the Deuteronomic History's first-cycle-close statement — the structural moment at which the Joshua-generation's loyalty-record (Josh 24:31's "all the days of Joshua, and all the days of the elders that overlived Joshua") ends. The "knew not the LORD" notation is not lack-of-information but lack-of-covenantal-knowledge; the next generation has not personally experienced the LORD's redemptive acts and has not been formed by them. The notation prepares the cyclic-pattern's installation that follows.

**The cyclic-pattern's programmatic statement** (2:11-23). The chapter's structural climax installs the framework that interprets the rest of the book. Six stages compose the pattern:

- *Apostasy* at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={2} verse="11-13" />: "And the children of Israel did evil in the sight of the LORD, and served Baalim... And they forsook the LORD, and served Baal and Ashtaroth."
- *Oppression* at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={2} verse="14-15" />: "And the anger of the LORD was hot against Israel, and he delivered them into the hands of spoilers that spoiled them, and he sold them into the hands of their enemies round about, so that they could not any longer stand before their enemies."
- *Distress* implied at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={2} verse={18} />: "...for it repented the LORD because of their groanings by reason of them that oppressed them and vexed them."
- *Deliverance* at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={2} verse={16} />: "Nevertheless the LORD raised up judges, which delivered them out of the hand of those that spoiled them."
- *Rest during the judge's tenure* at 2:18 (second half): "...then the LORD was with the judge, and delivered them out of the hand of their enemies all the days of the judge..."
- *Renewed apostasy after the judge dies* at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={2} verse={19} />: "And it came to pass, when the judge was dead, that they returned, and corrupted themselves more than their fathers, in following other gods to serve them, and to bow down unto them; they ceased not from their own doings, nor from their stubborn way."

The pattern's distinctive feature is the deteriorative trajectory marked at 2:19 by "more than their fathers" — each cycle is worse than the one before. This is not cyclic-equilibrium but cyclic-decline; the book's interpretive framework is downward-spiraling, not steady-state-repeating. The chapter closes at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={2} verse="20-23" /> with the LORD's framework-decision: "I also will not henceforth drive out any from before them of the nations which Joshua left when he died: That through them I may prove Israel, whether they will keep the way of the LORD to walk therein, as their fathers did keep it, or not." The remnant-nations are now installed as the LORD's instrument of testing — the territorial-incomplete framework's positive-pedagogical interpretation alongside its negative-consequence interpretation.

**The cyclic-pattern installed: Judges 2 as the book's interpretive framework + the Joshua 23:13 → Judges 2:3 fulfillment-trajectory closure.** The chapter operates at multiple register-levels simultaneously: it executes a specific cross-book prophetic-warning, it bridges the Joshua-period to the Judges-period at the level of named individuals and place-names, and it installs the cyclic-pattern framework that governs the entire rest of the book. Three architectural moves install the framework:

(1) **The Joshua 23:13 → Judges 2:3 fulfillment-trajectory closes within seven chapters.** Joshua 23:13 (Session 23 Sub-Batch 5 LangNote anticipated this trajectory explicitly): "Know for a certainty that the LORD your God will no more drive out any of these nations from before you; but they shall be snares and traps unto you, and scourges in your sides, and thorns in your eyes, until ye perish from off this good land." Judges 2:3, delivered by the angel of the LORD at Bochim: "I will not drive them out from before you; but they shall be as thorns in your sides, and their gods shall be a snare unto you." The two passages share the same core structural-claim ("I will not drive them out" + "they shall be thorns + a snare"). The Judges passage compresses the Joshua passage and shifts the framing toward the Bochim scene's specific covenantal-lawsuit form — but the prophetic-warning's identity is preserved. The fulfillment-trajectory is the deuteronomistic-loop's first explicit cross-book closure: Joshua's farewell-warning is narratively executed within the first three chapters of the next book. The Pentateuchal anchor at Num 33:55 ("those which ye let remain of them shall be pricks in your eyes, and thorns in your sides") and Exod 23:33 ("they shall not dwell in thy land, lest they make thee sin against me: for if thou serve their gods, it will surely be a snare unto thee") — both fetch-verified in the Session 23 Josh 23 LangNote — are the deeper canonical-trajectory anchors that Joshua 23 and Judges 2 jointly execute.

(2) **The Joshua-death recall at 2:6-10 ↔ Joshua 24:29-31 overlap-pattern.** The two passages share core content: Israel served the LORD all the days of Joshua and the elders who outlived Joshua; Joshua died at 110 years (eved YHWH title applied in both); burial in his inheritance in mount Ephraim. The one notable variation: Joshua 24:30 reads "Timnath-serah" (תִּמְנַת-סֶרַח) while Judges 2:9 reads "Timnath-heres" (תִּמְנַת-חֶרֶס) — same place, with the consonants of the second element reversed. Standard commentary treats this as scribal-tradition variation, with rabbinic interpretive traditions reading the *heres* form (related to the word for "sun") as connected to a sun-related etymology and the *serah* form as the original or vice versa. The variation is one of the OT-canon's recognized cross-book textual-tradition complexities; SumBible reports it without arbitration. The structural function of the overlap-pattern: the looking-back from Judges 2's framework-installation-moment toward the Joshua-period's close establishes the temporal-and-personnel ground on which the cyclic-pattern is being installed — *this* is the generation that has now arisen knowing not the LORD; the prior generation, which had served the LORD, has now passed away.

(3) **The cyclic-pattern as the Deuteronomistic-History's structural-framework anchor.** The cyclic-pattern installed at 2:11-23 is more than a description of the Judges narrative-content — it is the Deuteronomistic-History's structural-interpretive framework. On the Noth framework, the same deteriorative-cyclic logic governs the Samuel-Kings material (the king-by-king covenantal-evaluation pattern that culminates in the exile). The Judges-cycle's six stages (apostasy → oppression → distress → deliverance → rest → renewed apostasy) operate at the judges-period scale; the Kings-cycle's parallel pattern operates at the monarchy-period scale; in both, the deteriorative trajectory marked at 2:19's "more than their fathers" is the framework's distinctive contribution. The book of Judges' programmatic statement at 2:11-23 is therefore the DtrH's interpretive-key-statement for everything from Judges through 2 Kings 25 (the exile).

The 2:22 "that through them I may prove Israel" notation introduces a positive-pedagogical interpretation of the remnant-nations' continued presence alongside the negative-consequence interpretation. The remnant-nations are *both* the consequence of Israel's incomplete obedience *and* the LORD's instrument of ongoing testing. The dual register is theologically significant: the LORD's faithfulness to the covenantal framework includes pedagogical-testing of each generation, not just initial-gift delivery. The remnant-nations' presence functions analogously to the wilderness-testing framework (Deut 8:2's "to humble thee, and to prove thee") — the same covenantal-pedagogy logic applied to the post-conquest period. Subsequent judge-cycles will instantiate this testing-pedagogy across multiple oppressor-and-deliverer narratives.
