Judges 15 narrates the Samson cycle's central vengeance sequence: the foxes-and-firebrands, the Judah delegation, the jawbone slaughter, and the cycle's twenty-year tenure-notation. Six movements compose the chapter: Samson's return + the wife given to companion (15:1-2); the foxes destruction (15:3-5); the Philistine retaliation (15:6); the Etam slaughter and retreat (15:7-8); the Judah delegation and binding (15:9-13); the Spirit-empowerment and jawbone slaughter (15:14-17); Samson's thirst + the LORD providing water + the twenty-year notation (15:18-20).

**Samson's return + the wife given to companion** (15:1-2). The chapter opens at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={15} verse={1} /> after the wedding-feast aftermath of Judg 14:20: Samson returns to his wife "in the time of wheat harvest" with a kid — the canonical-narrative's marriage-restoration-attempt gesture. Her father refuses entry and explains at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={15} verse={2} />: "I verily thought that thou hadst utterly hated her; therefore I gave her to thy companion: is not her younger sister fairer than she?" The substitution-offer registers the social-political resolution the father attempts; Samson's response at 15:3 — "Now shall I be more blameless than the Philistines, though I do them a displeasure" — registers his interpretation of the act as Philistine-collective rather than individual-marital.

**The foxes-and-firebrands** (15:3-5). The destruction-mechanism at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={15} verse="4-5" />: "And Samson went and caught three hundred foxes, and took firebrands, and turned tail to tail, and put a firebrand in the midst between two tails. And when he had set the brands on fire, he let them go into the standing corn of the Philistines, and burnt up both the shocks, and also the standing corn, with the vineyards and olives." Three categories of agricultural-economy are burned simultaneously — grain, vineyards, olives — the Philistine harvest-economy's three-fold foundation. The act is targeted-economic-warfare at the staple-foundation register; the canonical narrative records the act without authorial-evaluation.

**The Philistine retaliation** (15:6). The Philistines' response at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={15} verse={6} /> — "Then the Philistines said, Who hath done this? And they answered, Samson, the son in law of the Timnite, because he had taken his wife, and given her to his companion. And the Philistines came up, and burnt her and her father with fire" — fulfills the threat the wedding companions had made at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={14} verse={15} /> (Sub-Batch 2 prior chapter): "lest we burn thee and thy father's house with fire." The threat the companions had used to extract the riddle is now executed by the Philistines themselves on the same wife. The canonical narrative records the causal-chain — companions' threat → wife's disclosure → Samson's Ashkelon-slaughter (14:19) → wife given to companion (14:20) → Samson's foxes destruction (15:4-5) → Philistine burning of wife and father (15:6) — without authorial-evaluation; the chain is the chapter's text-internal interpretive data.

**The Etam slaughter + retreat** (15:7-8). Samson's vengeance at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={15} verse="7-8" />: "Though ye have done this, yet will I be avenged of you, and after that I will cease. And he smote them hip and thigh with a great slaughter: and he went down and dwelt in the top of the rock Etam." The "hip and thigh" phrase registers the slaughter's comprehensiveness; the retreat to Etam sets up the Judah delegation that follows.

**The Judah delegation + binding** (15:9-13). The chapter's distinctive asymmetric-warfare scene comes at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={15} verse="11" />: "Then three thousand men of Judah went to the top of the rock Etam, and said to Samson, Knowest thou not that the Philistines are rulers over us? what is this that thou hast done unto us?" The 15:11 notation — "the Philistines are rulers over us" — is the canonical narrative's clearest pre-monarchic statement of Israelite vassalage. The 3,000-man delegation operates as Philistine-compliance enforcement against Samson rather than as Israelite-resistance against the Philistines; the cycle's structural-irony is that Judah does the Philistines' binding-work (see LangNote below).

Samson's negotiation with Judah at 15:12-13 secures the only term he requires: "Swear unto me, that ye will not fall upon me yourselves." Judah agrees to bind him without killing; they bind him "with two new cords" and bring him to Lehi for delivery to the Philistines.

**The Spirit-empowerment + jawbone slaughter** (15:14-17). The deliverance-from-binding comes at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={15} verse="14" />: "And when he came unto Lehi, the Philistines shouted against him: and the Spirit of the LORD came mightily upon him, and the cords that were upon his arms became as flax that was burnt with fire, and his bands loosed from off his hands." The Spirit-empowerment formula's third instance in the cycle (after 14:6 lion-rending and 14:19 Ashkelon-slaughter) maintains the parallel Spirit-empowerment + Nazirite-violation thread structure (see Judg 14 LangNote, Sub-Batch 2 prior chapter). The jawbone slaughter at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={15} verse="15-16" />: "And he found a new jawbone of an ass, and put forth his hand, and took it, and slew a thousand men therewith. And Samson said, With the jawbone of an ass, heaps upon heaps, with the jaw of an ass have I slain a thousand men." The fresh-jawbone corpse-contact is the cycle's second explicit Nazirite-vow violation (Num 6:6-7 prohibits corpse-contact); the chapter records the act without commenting on the violation. The Ramath-lehi naming at 15:17 closes the slaughter scene.

**Samson's thirst + water from the jaw + twenty years** (15:18-20). Samson's call on the LORD at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={15} verse="18" /> — "Thou hast given this great deliverance into the hand of thy servant: and now shall I die for thirst, and fall into the hand of the uncircumcised?" — registers the narrative's deliverance-attribution explicitly: the slaughter was the LORD's, not Samson's own. The LORD's response at 15:19: "But God clave an hollow place that was in the jaw, and there came water thereout; and when he had drunk, his spirit came again, and he revived." The Enhakkore ("spring of the caller") naming closes the scene. The chapter's closing-tenure notation at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={15} verse="20" />: "And he judged Israel in the days of the Philistines twenty years." Two structural notations: the tenure is shorter than Othniel/Ehud/Deborah-Barak/Gideon's forty- or eighty-year tenures; the qualifier "in the days of the Philistines" registers that the Philistine oppression continues unabated through Samson's judgeship — the deliverance is incomplete, consistent with Judg 13:5's "he shall *begin* to deliver Israel" announcement.

**The Judah-delegation scene + the asymmetric-warfare register + the pre-monarchic vassalage statement.** The Judg 15:9-13 scene is the canonical narrative's clearest single statement of Israelite vassalage to the Philistines in the pre-monarchic period. Three structural elements compose the scene's significance:

(1) **The 15:11 vassalage notation.** "Knowest thou not that the Philistines are rulers over us?" — Judah's spokesmen ask Samson the rhetorical question with the answer already presupposed. The notation registers Judah's tribal-political accommodation to Philistine rule as the known-and-acknowledged starting condition. The 40-year Philistine oppression announced at Judg 13:1 (Sub-Batch 1 prior chapter) is operating at the practical-political register: Judah accepts the Philistine rulership as fact, not contests it.

(2) **The 3,000-man Judah delegation operates as Philistine-compliance enforcement.** Where the prior major-judge cycles' tribal-musters had operated as Israelite-resistance against the foreign-oppressor (Barak's 10,000 against Sisera at Judg 4; Gideon's reduction from 22,000 to 300 against Midian at Judg 7), the Judah 3,000 here operate against the Israelite-resistance-figure (Samson) for the foreign-oppressor's benefit. The cycle's structural-inversion: the Judah delegation does the Philistines' binding-work on Samson. The chapter at hand's deliverance-from-binding at 15:14 (Spirit-empowerment, cords-as-flax) is therefore a double-deliverance: from the Philistines and from his own people's compliance. The canonical narrative carries the double-deliverance without authorial-evaluation; the text-internal data is the firm ground.

(3) **The cycle's broader "begin to deliver" framework.** Judg 13:5's announcement framework — "he shall *begin* to deliver Israel out of the hand of the Philistines" — operates at the chapter at hand's vassalage notation. Samson's deliverance is acknowledged in his cycle as incomplete; the 15:20 "in the days of the Philistines" qualifier registers the Philistine oppression as continuing across Samson's twenty-year judgeship. The framework's resolution comes only at the early-monarchic period (1 Samuel narratives) when the Philistine framework is broken by Samuel + Saul + David's cumulative action.

The form-resonance with Hebrews 11:34's "out of weakness were made strong, waxed valiant in fight, turned to flight the armies of the aliens" (fetch-verified Session 24 SB3) applies here as it did at Gideon (Session 25 SB2) and Jephthah (Session 27 SB2). Heb 11:32's cluster-framework reading of Samson at the faith-register operates on the 15:18 "great deliverance into the hand of thy servant" call-on-the-LORD register; the cluster's full faith-register vs moral-evaluation-register LangNote is reserved for the Judg 16 EXTRA-CARE chapter (Sub-Batch 3 next) where the framework's sharpest single-figure instance lands at the Philistine-temple closure.
