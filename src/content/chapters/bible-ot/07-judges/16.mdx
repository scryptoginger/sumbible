Judges 16 closes the Samson cycle and, with it, the major-judge cycles before the appendix-narratives (Judg 17-21, future-session). Four movements compose the chapter: the Gaza-doors episode (16:1-3); the Delilah cycle (16:4-22); the Philistine-temple closure (16:23-30); the burial-and-tenure notation (16:31).

**The Gaza-doors episode** (16:1-3). The chapter opens at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={16} verse={1} />: "Then went Samson to Gaza, and saw there an harlot, and went in unto her." The Gazites plot a dawn-ambush ("In the morning, when it is day, we shall kill him" — 16:2); Samson rises at midnight at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={16} verse={3} />: "took the doors of the gate of the city, and the two posts, and went away with them, bar and all, and put them upon his shoulders, and carried them up to the top of an hill that is before Hebron." The 40-mile carriage of the Gaza city-gate to a hill overlooking Hebron is a display of supernatural strength at the cycle's continuing Spirit-empowerment register. The episode's brief positioning prepares the Delilah cycle that follows.

**The Delilah cycle** (16:4-22). The chapter introduces Delilah at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={16} verse={4} />: "he loved a woman in the valley of Sorek, whose name was Delilah." The Philistine lords' offer at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={16} verse={5} /> — "we will give thee every one of us eleven hundred pieces of silver" — totals at least 5,500 shekels of silver from the five Philistine lords combined; the financial incentive is the cycle's clearest single instance of the silver-as-coup-financing register (parallel to Judg 9:4's Baalberith silver financing Abimelech's coup, drafted Session 26 SB1).

The four-stage seduction structures the cycle's central narrative:

- *First stage (16:6-9)*: seven green withs that were never dried. Samson breaks them "as a thread of tow is broken when it toucheth the fire."
- *Second stage (16:10-12)*: new ropes that never were occupied. Samson breaks them "from off his arms like a thread."
- *Third stage (16:13-14)*: weaving the seven locks of his head with the web + fastening with the pin. Samson awakes and goes "away with the pin of the beam, and with the web."
- *Fourth stage (16:15-17)*: Delilah's daily pressing — "she pressed him daily with her words, and urged him, so that his soul was vexed unto death" (16:16). Samson tells her all his heart at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={16} verse={17} />: "There hath not come a razor upon mine head; for I have been a <LangNote term="nazir" script="נָזִיר" language="Hebrew" gloss="‘Consecrated one’ / ‘set apart one,’ from the root nzr (to separate / consecrate). The Nazirite-vow framework is installed at Num 6:1-21; the perpetual-and-prenatal Samson variant is installed at Judg 13:5-7 (drafted Sub-Batch 1 prior chapter). The chapter at hand's 16:17 statement is the Samson cycle's explicit identification of the no-razor element as the vow's strength-bearing component — the canonical narrative does not present the strength as a magical property of the hair itself but as the LORD's empowerment operating in connection with the vow's preservation. The hair-as-token framework is the cycle's distinctive interpretive structure; the 16:20 'he wist not that the LORD was departed from him' notation makes the underlying LORD-presence operative-not-static.">Nazarite</LangNote> unto God from my mother's womb: if I be shaven, then my strength will go from me."

Delilah's call of the Philistine lords (16:18); the man brought to shave the seven locks while Samson sleeps on her knees (16:19); the strength's departure. The chapter's structural-pivot notation comes at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={16} verse={20} />: "And she said, The Philistines be upon thee, Samson. And he awoke out of his sleep, and said, I will go out as at other times before, and shake myself. And he wist not that the LORD was departed from him." The "he wist not" is the narrator's text-internal evaluation — Samson's expectation of the cycle's prior Spirit-empowerment continuing is broken at this scene; the cycle's deliverance-empowerment has departed (see LangNote below on the Spirit-coming / Spirit-departing framework).

The Philistines' capture at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={16} verse={21} />: "But the Philistines took him, and put out his eyes, and brought him down to Gaza, and bound him with fetters of brass; and he did grind in the prison house." The hinge-position notation at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={16} verse={22} /> opens the cycle's closure: "Howbeit the hair of his head began to grow again after he was shaven." The notation does not state that the LORD returned — only that the hair grew. The interpretive framework for what happens next is reserved for the Philistine-temple closure scene.

**The Philistine-temple closure** (16:23-30). The Dagon-temple sacrifice scene at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={16} verse={23} />: "Then the lords of the Philistines gathered them together for to offer a great sacrifice unto <LangNote term="Dagon" script="דָּגוֹן" language="Hebrew" gloss="The Philistine deity at Ashdod, Gaza, and other Philistine city-cult-centers. The deity's name etymology has been variously proposed (grain-fish-derivation debates among lexicographers); SumBible does not arbitrate. The Judg 16:23-31 scene's distinctive contribution to the Dagon framework: the celebratory sacrifice and the narrative's destruction-by-Samson registers the LORD's deliverance operating at the Dagon-temple's own celebration register — the cult-victory-narrative the Philistines stage becomes the LORD's-deliverance-narrative the canonical narrative records.">Dagon</LangNote> their god, and to rejoice." The Philistine cult-victory-celebration register operates explicitly: "Our god hath delivered Samson our enemy into our hand" (16:23 + 16:24 doubled). Samson is brought out to make sport (16:25); the lad leading him places his hands on the two middle pillars at his request (16:26); the house is full — "all the lords of the Philistines were there; and there were upon the roof about three thousand men and women, that beheld while Samson made sport" (16:27).

Samson's prayer at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={16} verse={28} />: "O Lord GOD, remember me, I pray thee, and strengthen me, I pray thee, only this once, O God, that I may be at once avenged of the Philistines for my two eyes." The prayer's structural elements — *remember me* + *strengthen me* + *only this once* + *avenged of the Philistines for my two eyes* — register both the call-on-the-LORD and the human-vengeance-motive simultaneously. The chapter's text-internal data carries both; the believer-spectrum reads them across multiple positions (see LangNote 1 below).

The temple's destruction at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={16} verse="29-30" />: "And Samson took hold of the two middle pillars upon which the house stood... And Samson said, Let me die with the Philistines. And he bowed himself with all his might; and the house fell upon the lords, and upon all the people that were therein. So the dead which he slew at his death were more than they which he slew in his life." The chapter's own narrative-evaluation at 16:30 — *the dead which he slew at his death were more than they which he slew in his life* — operates as the text-internal interpretation of the act's deliverance-significance; the four-position spectrum reads this data without canonical-arbitration (LangNote 1 below).

**The burial-and-tenure notation** (16:31). The cycle closes at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={16} verse={31} />: "Then his brethren and all the house of his father came down, and took him, and brought him up, and buried him between Zorah and Eshtaol in the buryingplace of Manoah his father. And he judged Israel twenty years." The burial returns Samson to the territory where the Spirit first began to move him (Judg 13:25 — "in the camp of Dan between Zorah and Eshtaol"); the geographic-circular closure registers the cycle's structural-completion. The twenty-year tenure is recorded for the second time in the same cycle (also at 15:20 — Sub-Batch 2 prior chapter); Samson is the only judge whose tenure is recorded twice. The structural-repetition prepares the appendix-narrative framework's structural-discontinuity — after Samson, no major-judge cycle follows.

**The Philistine-temple closure at Judg 16:23-31 — four-position sober-handling spectrum reported without arbitration.** The text-internal data is the firm ground: the 16:28 prayer (*remember me* + *strengthen me* + *only this once* + *avenged of the Philistines for my two eyes*); the 16:30 declaration (*Let me die with the Philistines*); the chapter's own narrative-evaluation at 16:30 (*the dead which he slew at his death were more than they which he slew in his life*); the cycle's prior Spirit-empowerment thread (14:6, 14:19, 15:14). Four position-types operate across the believer-spectrum:

(1) **Heroic-deliverance-by-Spirit reading.** The 16:28 prayer is a call-on-the-LORD with the deliverance-formula language (*remember*, *strengthen*) characteristic of OT prayer-of-faith; the LORD answers; the deliverance-outcome at 16:30 ends the cycle's Philistine-oppression-phase at significant scale. The "dead more than... in his life" notation registers the act at the divine-providence register — the deliverance is the LORD's, not Samson's strength alone. Some Christian commentary holds this position to read Samson at the Heb 11:32 faith-register tightly: faith called on the LORD at the moment of greatest weakness; the LORD answered.

(2) **Morally-problematic-suicide reading.** The act at 16:30 includes Samson's own death by his own action — *Let me die with the Philistines* + the pulling-down of the pillars while standing under them. Some Christian and Jewish commentary positions read this as a suicide-act distinguishable from divinely-empowered deliverance; the moral-evaluation register operates against the act regardless of the deliverance-outcome. The cycle's prior moral-complexity (the Timnathite-wife pursuit, the Gaza-harlot, the Delilah cycle) compounds the position's reading.

(3) **Sacred-narrative-recording-without-endorsing reading.** Parallel to the Ehud / Jael / Abimelech / Jephthah's daughter sober-handling spectra (Sessions 24 + 26 + 27): the canonical canvas records the historical-political reality of the Philistine-temple closure without underwriting every operational detail. The chapter's text-internal evaluation at 16:30 (the dead-more-than-in-his-life notation) carries the deliverance-significance at the historical-political register; the moral-evaluation question of the act's methodology operates at a different register the canonical narrative does not arbitrate.

(4) **ANE warrior-honor framework reading.** "Let me die with the Philistines" at 16:30 operates at the ANE warrior-honor register where dying-while-defeating-the-enemy is the cycle's structural-closure rather than a moral-evaluation question. The framework reads the act as the warrior-honor cycle's terminal-form within the ANE cultural-and-historical context the canonical narrative records; the modern moral-evaluation categories that the believer-spectrum's other positions operate with are not the framework's primary register.

SumBible reports the spectrum without arbitration per §0 Prime Directive #5 — consistent with Sessions 24 + 26 + 27 + Sub-Batch 1 prior chapter (Judg 13:18 *peli* reception-history) discipline. The four positions read the text-internal data at different registers; the canonical narrative carries the spectrum forward without resolving it.

**Hebrews 11:32 cluster's fourth and final member handling — faith-register vs moral-evaluation-register distinction at its sharpest single-figure instance + cluster pattern complete.** Samson is the fourth member of the Hebrews 11:32 cluster ("Gedeon, and... Barak, and... Samson, and of Jephthae" — fetch-verified Session 24 SB3). With the chapter at hand, the cluster pattern is complete: all four members drafted across Sessions 24 (Barak) + 25 (Gedeon) + 27 (Jephthae) + 28 (Samson). The Hebrews-author's selection-discipline across the cluster's four members is consistent:

- **Gideon (Session 25 SB2)** — Heb 11:32 faith-register reads the 300-against-Midian deliverance act (Judg 7); moral-evaluation register reads the ephod-compromise (Judg 8:24-27, *"which thing became a snare unto Gideon, and to his house"*).
- **Barak (Session 24 SB3)** — Heb 11:32 faith-register reads the going-against-iron-chariots-at-Deborah's-word act (Judg 4); moral-evaluation register reads the conditional-going request at Judg 4:8.
- **Jephthae (Session 27 SB2)** — Heb 11:32 faith-register reads the Spirit-empowered Ammon-conquest (Judg 11:29-33); moral-evaluation register reads the vow-and-execution at Judg 11:30-40.
- **Samson (the chapter at hand)** — Heb 11:32 faith-register reads the 16:28 prayer and 16:30 outcome; moral-evaluation register reads the cycle's Nazirite-violations + Gaza-harlot + Delilah cycle + the suicide-act dimension of 16:30.

The Samson instance is the cluster's sharpest single-figure tension because the faith-act — Samson's call on the LORD at 16:28 + the Philistine-temple closure at 16:30 — is simultaneously the moral-complexity-event. Where the Gideon, Barak, and Jephthah cases have the faith-act and the moral-complexity-event at different points in the narrative, the Samson case has them at the same scene. The cluster pattern's structural-significance: the Hebrews-author selects four Judges-figures whose moral-complexity the canonical OT narrative carries forward without arbitration, reads them at the faith-register that operates orthogonally to the moral-evaluation register. Two registers operate on the same person at different evaluation-axes; canonical narrative carries both as legitimate without resolution. The reading is theological-typological at the NT-canonical-redaction level, not exegetical-arbitration of the OT-narrative's evaluation.

The Heb 11:34 general description ("out of weakness were made strong, waxed valiant in fight, turned to flight the armies of the aliens" — fetch-verified Session 24 SB3) fits the Samson cycle at multiple points: "out of weakness were made strong" fits 16:21-22 (eyes put out + Gaza-prison-mill grinding + hair growing back) → 16:28-30 (the prayer-and-deliverance at the temple's collapse); "waxed valiant in fight" fits the lion-rending + Ashkelon-slaughter + foxes-destruction + jawbone-slaughter pattern across Judg 14-15; "turned to flight the armies of the aliens" fits the cycle's broader Philistine-resistance arc, though the Judg 13:5 "BEGIN to deliver Israel" announcement framework — Samson's deliverance is acknowledged in his cycle as incomplete — qualifies the application. The Heb 11:34 description applies to the cluster's four members in different specific instantiations; the cluster's broader pattern is the Hebrews-author's selection of figures whose faith-register reading registers at multiple narrative-points without requiring single-act-of-faith specification.

**The 16:20 Spirit-departure notation + the Spirit-coming / Spirit-departing framework across the Samson cycle.** The narrator's 16:20 evaluation — *And he wist not that the LORD was departed from him* — is one of the OT's most pointed text-internal evaluations of divine-presence departure. The notation operates at the cycle's structural-pivot:

| Verse | Spirit-event | Register |
|---|---|---|
| 13:25 | "the Spirit of the LORD began to move him at times" | initial Spirit-coming |
| 14:6 | "the Spirit of the LORD came mightily upon him" (lion) | deliverance-empowerment |
| 14:19 | "the Spirit of the LORD came upon him" (Ashkelon) | deliverance-empowerment |
| 15:14 | "the Spirit of the LORD came mightily upon him" (jawbone) | deliverance-empowerment |
| 16:20 | "he wist not that the LORD was departed from him" | **Spirit-departure** |
| 16:22 | "the hair of his head began to grow again" | hinge-position (no LORD-return stated) |
| 16:28 | "O Lord GOD, remember me... strengthen me... only this once" | call-on-the-LORD (fresh request, not presupposition) |
| 16:30 | "the dead which he slew at his death were more than they which he slew in his life" | deliverance-outcome (LORD's empowerment register implicit) |

The cycle's structural arc registers the divine-providence framework as operative-and-conditional rather than as static-empowerment. The Spirit-comes / LORD-departs / hair-grows / prayer-of-call / deliverance-act sequence shows the Spirit-empowerment framework as related to the Nazirite-vow's preservation but not magically determined by the hair itself — the 16:17 statement ("if I be shaven, then my strength will go from me") functions at the vow-token register, where the hair's preservation operates as the visible-sign of the consecration the LORD's empowerment is connected to.

The cross-corpus parallel at 1 Samuel 16:14 (the Spirit of the LORD departing from Saul) + 16:23 + 18:10 (the evil spirit upon Saul) registers the same Spirit-coming / Spirit-departing framework at a parallel pre-monarchic cycle. SumBible notes the form-resonance without claiming direct dependence either direction — the framework's recurrence across the Samson cycle and the Saul cycle registers the divine-providence framework as operative across the canonical narrative's pre-monarchic and early-monarchic transitions, with the Saul cycle developing the framework further at the King-Saul Spirit-coming / Spirit-departing register the chapter at hand prepares.
