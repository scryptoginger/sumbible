Judges 18 continues the appendix-narrative block opened at Judg 17 and narratively-executes the Dan-relocation cross-corpus arc that began at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={19} verse={47} /> (drafted Session 23 SB3). The chapter opens with the framing-formula's shortened form — "In those days there was no king in Israel" — re-installed at the appendix block's second narrative; the Judg 17:6 full-form formula's interpretive-key continues to govern the reading.

Five movements compose the chapter: the framing-formula and the Danites' search for inheritance (18:1); the five Danite spies at Micah's house and the Levite-priest's blessing (18:2-6); the Laish report (18:7-10); the six-hundred-Danites' taking of the images and the priest from Micah (18:11-26); and the Laish-conquest with the cult-installation closure (18:27-31).

**The framing-formula re-installed + the Danites' search for inheritance** (18:1). The chapter opens at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={18} verse={1} />: "In those days there was no king in Israel: and in those days the tribe of the Danites sought them an inheritance to dwell in; for unto that day all their inheritance had not fallen unto them among the tribes of Israel." The framing-formula's shortened form re-installs the appendix-narrative block's interpretive-key from 17:6; the "had not fallen unto them" notation is the chapter's first explicit invocation of the territorial-insufficiency framework that Josh 19:47 had forward-referenced and Judg 1:34-36 had developed (see LangNote below on the Dan-relocation cross-corpus arc).

**The five Danite spies at Micah's house** (18:2-6). The chapter records at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={18} verse={2} /> that "the children of Dan sent of their family five men from their coasts, men of valour, from Zorah, and from Eshtaol, to spy out the land, and to search it." The Zorah-and-Eshtaol territorial-anchor is the same geographic frame from which the Spirit of the LORD first began to move Samson (Judg 13:25 — "in the camp of Dan between Zorah and Eshtaol"). The same territorial-base now sends out the spy-party that will inaugurate the tribe's full northward migration.

At Micah's house the spies recognize the Levite-priest's voice (18:3) and ask him to inquire of God; the priest's response at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={18} verse={6} /> — "Go in peace: before the LORD is your way wherein ye go" — operates at the LORD-mediated-blessing register. The Levite-priest's prophetic-pretension on behalf of an illegitimate cult-installation is the structural-tension the chapter records without arbitration; the canonical narrative carries the priest's blessing forward without confirming or denying its prophetic-authenticity.

**The Laish report** (18:7-10). The spies reach Laish and describe it at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={18} verse={7} />: the inhabitants "dwelt careless, after the manner of the Zidonians, quiet and secure; and there was no magistrate in the land, that might put them to shame in any thing; and they were far from the Zidonians, and had no business with any man." The triple-characterization — *quiet and secure*, *no magistrate*, *far from the Zidonians* — establishes Laish as militarily vulnerable. The spies' report to the tribe at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={18} verse={9} /> urges immediate action: "Arise, that we may go up against them: for we have seen the land, and, behold, it is very good." The chapter's second God-language at 18:10 — "for God hath given it into your hands" — operates at the providence-claim register; the canonical narrative records the claim without arbitration of its theological-authenticity.

**The taking of the images and the priest** (18:11-26). Six hundred Danites with weapons of war depart from Zorah and Eshtaol (18:11); they pitch at Kirjathjearim, naming the place Mahanehdan ("camp of Dan") — a place-name notation that depends on the same Danite-migration the chapter narrates (18:12). At Micah's house the five spies re-enter and take "the graven image, and the ephod, and the teraphim, and the molten image" (<VerseRef book="judges" chapter={18} verse={17} />) while the six hundred armed men stand at the gate.

The Levite-priest's accession at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={18} verse={19} /> registers the chapter's clearest single moment of the priesthood-as-hired-service framework: "Hold thy peace, lay thine hand upon thy mouth, and go with us, and be to us a father and a priest: is it better for thee to be a priest unto the house of one man, or that thou be a priest unto a tribe and a family in Israel?" The Levite reasons at the social-status register — a tribe-and-family clientele exceeds a single-household clientele — and at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={18} verse={20} /> "the priest's heart was glad, and he took the ephod, and the teraphim, and the graven image, and went in the midst of the people." The Judg 17:10-12 hired-service framework (ten shekels of silver by the year, a suit of apparel, and victuals — see Session 29 SB1 prior chapter) now upgrades to tribe-and-family scale.

Micah's protest at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={18} verse={24} /> — "Ye have taken away my gods which I made, and the priest, and ye are gone away: and what have I more?" — names what has been taken in precise inverse-order from his Judg 17:5 inventory. The Danites' threat-of-force response at 18:25 ("Let not thy voice be heard among us, lest angry fellows run upon thee, and thou lose thy life, with the lives of thy household") settles the matter at the realpolitik-of-force register the appendix-narrative block's "no king" framing-formula reads through.

**The Laish-conquest and the cult-installation closure** (18:27-31). The chapter records the conquest at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={18} verse={27} />: "they took the things which Micah had made, and the priest which he had, and came unto Laish, unto a people that were at quiet and secure: and they smote them with the edge of the sword, and burnt the city with fire." The no-deliverer notation at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={18} verse={28} /> ("there was no deliverer, because it was far from Zidon, and they had no business with any man") echoes the spies' 18:7 report and registers the conquest's success at the strategically-isolated-victim register.

The renaming at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={18} verse={29} /> closes the geographic-relocation: "they called the name of the city Dan, after the name of Dan their father, who was born unto Israel: howbeit the name of the city was Laish at the first." The notation is the explicit narrative-execution of Josh 19:47's Leshem/Laish-renamed-Dan forward-reference (see LangNote below); the northern Dan whose existence the "from Dan even to Beersheba" proverbial-formula depends on is now installed in the canonical narrative.

The cult-installation closure at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={18} verse={30} /> reads: "And the children of Dan set up the graven image: and Jonathan, the son of Gershom, the son of Manasseh, he and his sons were priests to the tribe of Dan until the day of the captivity of the land." The Jonathan-son-of-Gershom-son-of-Manasseh genealogy carries a textual-tradition complexity SumBible reports without arbitration: rabbinic textual-tradition records a suspended nun in מנשה ("Manasseh") that, without the suspended letter, reads משה ("Moses") — see LangNote below. The "until the day of the captivity of the land" notation provides a chronological-anchor for the cult-installation's duration (standard commentary debates whether this refers to the Assyrian-conquest of the northern kingdom in 722 BC or to the earlier Philistine-capture of the ark and the Shiloh-sanctuary's destruction — SumBible reports the framework without arbitration). The chapter's final notation at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={18} verse={31} /> closes both temporal-frames jointly: "And they set them up Micah's graven image, which he made, all the time that the house of God was in Shiloh."

**The Dan-relocation cross-corpus arc completed — Josh 19:47 → Judg 1:34-36 → Judg 5:17 → Judg 18, and the 'from Dan to Beersheba' proverbial-formula's geographic-anchor.** The chapter at hand executes the narrative-resolution of a four-stage cross-corpus arc the Pentateuchal-and-Joshua framework anticipated. Four anchor-points trace the arc:

(1) **<VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={19} verse="40-48" /> (drafted Session 23 SB3)** installs Dan's seventh-lot allotment in the central-western lowlands (Zorah, Eshtaol, Ekron, surrounding territory). The territorial-insufficiency notation at Josh 19:47 — Dan's coast "went out too little for them," prompting the migration to Leshem and the renaming after Dan their father — functions as the forward-reference Judg 18 will narratively-execute in fuller detail. The Josh 19:47 verse is itself a brief proleptic-notation of the same migration the chapter at hand records in full.

(2) **<VerseRef book="judges" chapter={1} verse="34-36" /> (drafted Session 24 SB1)** records the Amorites forcing the Danites into the mountain rather than letting them come down to the valley — installing the territorial-pressure that motivates the Judg 18 migration. The 1:34 notation is positioned at the tribe-by-tribe negative-enumeration's terminal position (after Benjamin, Manasseh, Ephraim, Zebulun, Asher, Naphtali — Dan closes the list), structurally registering Dan's territorial-distress as the most-acute case before the cyclic-pattern framework's installation at Judg 2.

(3) **<VerseRef book="judges" chapter={5} verse={17} /> (drafted Session 24 SB4)** records the Song of Deborah's tribal-participation roll-call's gentle-rebuke notation regarding Dan: "and why did Dan remain in ships?" The "remain in ships" phrase registers Dan's non-participation in the Kishon-victory at a tribal-loyalty register; the sea-coast positioning the verse implies depends on Dan's pre-migration coastal-territory orientation. After the chapter at hand's migration, this sea-coast positioning no longer applies — the northern Dan is inland, far from any coast.

(4) **Judges 18 (the chapter at hand)** records the migration's narrative-execution: five spies, the Levite-priest's blessing, the Laish-report, the six-hundred armed Danites, the taking of Micah's cult-objects and priest, the Laish-conquest, the renaming to Dan, the cult-installation. The chapter's structural-significance is geographic-historical: it installs the northern Dan whose existence is the precondition for the "from Dan to Beersheba" proverbial-formula's later canonical-narrative usage.

The "from Dan even to Beersheba" formula recurs across the canonical narrative as the proverbial-expression for the entire territorial-extent of Israel: <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={20} verse={1} /> (the all-Israel assembly at Mizpeh); <VerseRef book="1-samuel" chapter={3} verse={20} /> (Samuel as prophet recognized "from Dan even to Beersheba"); <VerseRef book="2-samuel" chapter={3} verse={10} /> (the kingdom-extent David is to receive); <VerseRef book="2-samuel" chapter={17} verse={11} /> (Hushai's all-Israel-gathered advice); <VerseRef book="2-samuel" chapter={24} verse={2} /> (David's census-extent); <VerseRef book="1-kings" chapter={4} verse={25} /> (Solomon's reign-extent — "from Dan even to Beersheba"); <VerseRef book="1-chronicles" chapter={21} verse={2} /> (the 1 Chr parallel to 2 Sam 24); <VerseRef book="2-chronicles" chapter={30} verse={5} /> (Hezekiah's Passover-proclamation extent). The proverbial-formula's geographic-historical-anchor depends on the northern Dan the chapter at hand installs; the prior southern-coastal Dan would not have generated the proverb's particular spatial-extent.

The chapter does not arbitrate the cult-installation's moral-evaluation register. The "no king" framing-formula's interpretive-key (Judg 17:6, drafted SB1 prior chapter) reads the Danite-migration's deteriorative-trajectory at the social-and-cultic-disorder register: Israel's tribes operate at the "right in his own eyes" diagnostic where the appendix-narrative block's tribal-territorial-relocation and cult-installation now register against the central-sanctuary framework Deut 12 + Josh 18:1 had installed.

**The Jonathan-son-of-Gershom-son-of-Manasseh/Moses textual-tradition note at Judg 18:30 reported without arbitration.** The MT reading at <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={18} verse={30} /> identifies the Levite-priest the Danites take from Micah's house as "Jonathan, the son of Gershom, the son of Manasseh." The Hebrew consonantal text at this point preserves a textual-tradition complexity: the name מנשה ("Manasseh") includes a *suspended nun* — a small nun (נ) written above the line rather than in the standard letter-row position. Without the suspended nun, the consonants read מ-ש-ה (משה, "Moses"). The rabbinic textual-tradition records the suspended-nun convention as a scribal-preservation of both readings:

- *Reading-with-the-suspended-nun*: "Jonathan, the son of Gershom, the son of Manasseh" — the Levite-priest is descended from Manasseh.
- *Reading-without-the-suspended-nun*: "Jonathan, the son of Gershom, the son of Moses" — the Levite-priest is descended from Moses through Moses' firstborn son Gershom (<VerseRef book="exodus" chapter={2} verse={22} /> + <VerseRef book="exodus" chapter={18} verse={3} />).

Rabbinic-tradition explanation (preserved in Talmud Bava Bathra 109b and in the BHS critical apparatus): the suspended-nun convention was a scribal-honor-protection of Moses' name — preserving the textual-witness that the priest at the illegitimate cult-installation was Moses' grandson, while signaling that the scribes were not willing to leave Moses' name standing without protective-marking at that genealogical-position. Many English translations follow the "Manasseh" reading (KJV, ASV, Geneva Bible) without flagging the suspended-nun convention; other translations note the "Moses" alternate in footnotes (NRSV, NIV, ESV, NET Bible).

SumBible reports the textual-tradition note without arbitration per §0 Prime Directive #5. The canonical narrative carries both readings forward; the moral-evaluation question of whether the underlying tradition recorded the priest as Moses' grandson or as a different Levite-genealogy operates at a register the canonical text does not explicitly resolve. The textual-tradition's existence — the suspended-nun convention itself — is the firm-ground claim; the historical-original-reading question operates beyond the firm-ground data and is left to standard commentary.
