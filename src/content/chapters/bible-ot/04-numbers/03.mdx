Numbers 3 turns from the twelve-tribe military census of chapter 1 to the Levites' separate count and their distinctive vocation. The chapter has four major movements: the lineage of Aaron with its brief return to the Nadab-and-Abihu catastrophe (3:1-4), the Levites' assignment to priestly service (3:5-10), the firstborn-substitution claim (3:11-13), and the Levite census proper, arranged by the three Levitical clans (3:14-39), with the closing redemption-calculation for the surplus firstborn (3:40-51).

**Aaron's lineage and the Nadab-Abihu pickup** (3:1-4). The chapter opens with the four sons of Aaron — Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, Ithamar — and immediately notes the Lev 10 catastrophe: <VerseRef book="numbers" chapter={3} verse="3-4" /> — "These are the names of the sons of Aaron, the priests which were anointed, whom he consecrated to minister in the priest's office. And Nadab and Abihu died before the LORD, when they offered strange fire before the LORD, in the wilderness of Sinai, and they had no children." The brief note is structurally significant: it explains why the high-priestly succession line will run through Eleazar (the third son) rather than Nadab (the eldest), as confirmed at <VerseRef book="numbers" chapter={20} verse="25-28" /> (Aaron's death, Eleazar's investiture). The chapter assumes Lev 10 is known and picks it up to clear the genealogical record for the rest of Numbers.

**The Levites assigned to priestly service** (3:5-10). The LORD instructs Moses to bring the tribe of Levi near and present them before Aaron the priest. The Levites do not themselves minister at the altar — that office belongs to Aaron and his sons alone (3:10 — "and the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death," a foreshadowing of Num 16's Korah revolt and its outcome). The Levites' role is rather to assist the priests: keeping the charge of the tabernacle, doing the transport-and-setup service, guarding against unauthorized approach.

**The firstborn-substitution** (3:11-13). The chapter's theological center, the basis for the Levites' separate identity: <VerseRef book="numbers" chapter={3} verse="12-13" /> — "I have taken the Levites from among the children of Israel instead of all the firstborn that openeth the matrix among the children of Israel: therefore the Levites shall be mine; Because all the firstborn are mine; for on the day that I smote all the firstborn in the land of Egypt I hallowed unto me all the firstborn in Israel, both man and beast: mine they shall be: I am the LORD." The Egypt-night of <VerseRef book="exodus" chapter={12} verse={29} /> is the chapter's foundation: God's claim on Israel's firstborn was established when His judgment passed over Israel's houses and fell on Egypt's. <LangNote term="bekor" script="בְּכוֹר" language="Hebrew" gloss="‘Firstborn.’ The Hebrew keyword for the OT's firstborn-vocabulary, running through the Genesis primogeniture stories (Esau and Jacob, Reuben's loss of firstborn rights), the Passover narrative (Exod 11:5, 12:29, the firstborn of Egypt), the firstborn-sanctification command (Exod 13:2, 22:29), and Israel's identity as YHWH's firstborn son (Exod 4:22 — ‘Israel is my son, even my firstborn’). The chapter's substitution of the Levites for the firstborn institutes the OT's most-extended single piece of firstborn-redemption legislation.">bekor</LangNote> belongs to YHWH; the Levites are the institutional way that claim is fulfilled.

**The three-clan Levite census** (3:14-39). Levi's three sons give their names to the three Levitical clans: <LangNote term="Gershon / Kohath / Merari" script="גֵּרְשׁוֹן / קְהָת / מְרָרִי" language="Hebrew" gloss="The three sons of Levi (Gen 46:11; Exod 6:16; Num 3:17). Gershon (eldest) — his clan camps west of the tabernacle and (per Num 4:24-26) carries the curtains and coverings. Kohath (middle) — his clan camps south, and from his branch Amram comes Aaron and Moses (Exod 6:18-20); the Kohathites carry the most-holy objects (ark, table, lampstand, altars — Num 4:4-15). Merari (youngest) — his clan camps north and (per Num 4:31-33) carries the structural frames, bars, pillars, and sockets. The three-clan division installs the OT priesthood's working organization.">Gershon, Kohath, Merari</LangNote>. The chapter records each clan's count (Gershonites 7,500, Kohathites 8,600, Merarites 6,200; total 22,000) and their camp position around the tabernacle: Gershonites on the west, Kohathites on the south, Merarites on the north. The east side is reserved for Moses, Aaron, and Aaron's sons (3:38) — "and the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death." The Levite arrangement forms the inner ring around the tabernacle, sitting inside the twelve-tribe outer ring of Num 2.

**The redemption of the surplus firstborn** (3:40-51). The chapter closes with a precise accounting. The Israelite firstborn males — counted by "every male from a month old and upward" — number 22,273. The Levites number 22,000. The surplus 273 firstborn require individual redemption at five shekels of the sanctuary apiece (3:47), a total of 1,365 shekels paid to Aaron and his sons. The chapter installs the OT's <LangNote term="pidyon ha-ben" script="פִּדְיוֹן הַבֵּן" language="Hebrew" gloss="‘Redemption of the [firstborn] son.’ The post-biblical Jewish ritual derived from Num 3:46-51 and Num 18:15-16. A firstborn son (not the firstborn of a Kohen or Levite family, and not delivered by Caesarean section) is ‘redeemed’ on the thirty-first day of life by his father paying five silver coins to a Kohen — the same five-shekel-of-the-sanctuary rate the chapter installs. The practice continues in observant Jewish communities to the present; it is one of the OT's longest-living single ritual institutions.">pidyon ha-ben</LangNote> framework — the five-shekel redemption-rate that continues in Jewish practice as a living institution to this day.

**The firstborn-substitution and the substitutionary-redemption pattern.** Numbers 3's substitution of the Levites for the Israelite firstborn is one of the OT's most-developed single substitution-theologies. The structural logic: God's claim on the firstborn (established on the Passover night when the firstborn of Egypt died and the firstborn of Israel were preserved by the blood on the doorposts, <VerseRef book="exodus" chapter={12} verse="29" />) is real and binding. Every Israelite firstborn male belongs to YHWH. But the LORD does not require every firstborn for sanctuary service; instead He institutes the Levites as a corporate substitute, with the surplus individually redeemed by the five-shekel payment. The pattern — divine claim, corporate substitute, monetary redemption — runs through the OT's atonement-and-redemption vocabulary. The NT carries it forward Christologically: Christ is "the firstborn from the dead" (<VerseRef book="colossians" chapter={1} verse={18} />) who substitutes for those under condemnation (cf. <VerseRef book="hebrews" chapter={2} verse="14-15" />); the believer is redeemed "not with corruptible things, as silver and gold... But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot" (<VerseRef book="1-peter" chapter={1} verse="18-19" />, picking up both the firstborn-redemption vocabulary and the Passover-lamb vocabulary). The chapter at hand installs the OT framework; the NT carries the redemption-payment-by-substitute pattern to its Christological terminus.

**The Nadab-Abihu pickup and the high-priestly succession.** Numbers 3:4's brief note that Nadab and Abihu died "before the LORD" picks up <VerseRef book="leviticus" chapter={10} verse="1-3" /> with structural significance for the book of Numbers. The death of Aaron's two eldest sons without progeny means the high-priestly line descends through Eleazar (Aaron's third son). The succession runs Aaron → Eleazar → Phinehas (cf. <VerseRef book="numbers" chapter={25} verse="10-13" />'s "everlasting priesthood" promise to Phinehas after the Baal-Peor episode). The chapter at hand registers the genealogical fact; the rest of Numbers operationalizes it. By <VerseRef book="numbers" chapter={20} verse="25-28" />, when Aaron dies on Mount Hor, the transition of vestments and office to Eleazar is performed without controversy — because Num 3:4 has already established Eleazar's position as the eldest surviving priestly son.

</LangNotes>
