Joshua 16 is the brief Ephraim allotment chapter — a transitional block between Judah's vast southern allotment (Josh 15) and Manasseh's allotment with the Zelophehad-daughters narrative (Josh 17). Three movements structure the chapter: Joseph's tribes' joint border opening (16:1-4); Ephraim's specific border, with the notation that Ephraim has cities scattered inside Manasseh's territory (16:5-9); and the candid closing notation that Ephraim did not drive out the Canaanites at Gezer (16:10).

**Joseph's tribes' joint border** (16:1-4). The chapter opens at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={16} verse="1-2" />: "And the lot of the children of Joseph fell from Jordan by Jericho, unto the water of Jericho on the east, to the wilderness that goeth up from Jericho throughout mount Bethel, And goeth out from Bethel to Luz." The Joseph allotment begins at Jericho — adjacent to Judah's northern border — and runs westward toward the sea. Ephraim and Manasseh are described jointly here because they together compose Joseph's inheritance per Jacob's adoption-blessing at Gen 48:5; their territories will be distinguished as the chapter continues and across into Joshua 17.

**Ephraim's specific border** (16:5-9). The chapter then narrows to Ephraim at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={16} verse="5" />: "And the border of the children of Ephraim according to their families was thus." The description traces Ephraim's eastern border from Atarothaddar to upper Bethhoron, and its western reach toward the sea. The notable single notation at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={16} verse="9" /> records the cross-tribal arrangement: "And the separate cities for the children of Ephraim were among the inheritance of the children of Manasseh, all the cities with their villages." Ephraim and Manasseh's territories interpenetrate — a territorial reflection of the brotherhood of Joseph.

**The Gezer notation** (16:10). The chapter closes at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={16} verse={10} />: "And they drave not out the Canaanites that dwelt in Gezer: but the Canaanites dwell among the Ephraimites unto this day, and serve under tribute." This candid not-fully-possessed notation parallels <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={15} verse={63} />'s Jebusites-at-Jerusalem notation drafted prior chapter, but adds a distinct element: the Canaanites at Gezer "serve under tribute." The broader OT trajectory for Gezer: <VerseRef book="judges" chapter={1} verse={29} /> records the same pattern at the Judges-narrative stage; <VerseRef book="1-kings" chapter={9} verse="16-17" /> records Pharaoh's later capture of Gezer, its gift to Solomon as his wife's dowry, and Solomon's rebuilding of the city.

<LangNotes>

**The Joseph double-portion — Gen 48 connection-back.** Joshua 16's allotment-for-Ephraim and Joshua 17's allotment-for-Manasseh together honor Jacob's adoption of Joseph's two sons at Gen 48:1-22. Three elements of that earlier scene shape the Joshua-allotment arithmetic:

(1) **Gen 48:5 — Jacob's adoption.** "And now thy two sons, Ephraim and Manasseh, which were born unto thee in the land of Egypt before I came unto thee into Egypt, are mine; as Reuben and Simeon, they shall be mine." Jacob grants Ephraim and Manasseh tribal status equal to Reuben and Simeon. They function as two tribes rather than one within the twelve-tribe count.

(2) **Gen 48:13-20 — the younger blessed above the elder.** Jacob crosses his hands at the blessing, placing his right hand on Ephraim (the younger) and his left on Manasseh (the elder). Joseph tries to correct him; Jacob refuses: "I know it, my son, I know it: he also shall become a people, and he also shall be great: but truly his younger brother shall be greater than he, and his seed shall become a multitude of nations" (Gen 48:19). Ephraim's covenantal priority over Manasseh is established at this scene; Joshua 16's installation of Ephraim's allotment *before* Manasseh's in Joshua 17 echoes the same ordering.

(3) **The twelve-tribe arithmetic preserved.** Levi receives no territorial inheritance (Josh 13:14, 33 — drafted prior chapter); Joseph receives two allotments. The structural arithmetic: nine cis-Jordan tribes (Judah + Ephraim + half-Manasseh-west + Benjamin + Simeon + Zebulun + Issachar + Asher + Naphtali + Dan) plus two-and-a-half east-Jordan tribes (Reuben + Gad + half-Manasseh-east) yields the twelve-tribe count exactly. Both the Levi exception and the Joseph double portion are required to make the arithmetic work.

The Ephraim priority established at Gen 48 continues across the OT historical and prophetic literature: Ephraim is the dominant tribe of the divided Northern Kingdom (1-2 Kings), and "Ephraim" frequently stands as prophetic shorthand for the northern kingdom as a whole (Hosea, Isaiah, Jeremiah). Joshua 16 records the territorial installation that grounds this longer arc.

**The Gezer notation — territorial-incomplete pattern with a tribute variant.** Joshua 16:10 sits within a recurring OT-historical pattern of territorial-incomplete notations across the allotment chapters and into Judges 1. The notations vary in their resolution-stage:

- **Coexistence without tribute** — Josh 15:63 (Jebusites at Jerusalem). No tribute arrangement is recorded at this stage.
- **Coexistence with tribute** — Josh 16:10 (Canaanites at Gezer). The Canaanites remain in place but serve under Israelite tribute — an intermediate resolution between full expulsion and full coexistence.
- **Later-narrative resolution** — the Jerusalem-Jebusite case is eventually resolved at 2 Sam 5:6-9 (David's capture of Jerusalem); the Gezer case is eventually resolved at 1 Kings 9:16-17 (Pharaoh's capture and Solomon's rebuilding).

These notations are the historical-literature's structural honesty about how incompletely the conquest-and-settlement narrative actually maps onto the territorial program. Joshua 11:23 had registered the conquest as in one sense "finished"; Josh 13:1 and the allotment-chapter notations register that in another sense — the actual-occupation sense — very much remained. The two registers are not in contradiction; they distinguish a conquest-and-distribution sense (complete) from an actual-occupation sense (incomplete) — the same register-distinction surfaced at Josh 13 drafted prior block.
