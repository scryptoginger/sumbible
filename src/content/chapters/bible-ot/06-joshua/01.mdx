Joshua 1 opens the Deuteronomic History. After Moses' death, the LORD speaks directly to Joshua and gives the foundational charge for entering the land. The chapter has three major movements: the LORD's direct charge to Joshua (1:1-9); Joshua's command to the officers and the eastern tribes' reminder of Moses' commission (1:10-15); the people's pledge of obedience with the chazaq-ve-amets formula's fifth-and-culminating occurrence at 1:18 (1:16-18). The chapter is the narrative-prophetic culmination of the Joshua-succession typological framework that <VerseRef book="numbers" chapter={27} verse="12-23" /> first installed and <VerseRef book="deuteronomy" chapter={31} verse="1-23" /> and <VerseRef book="deuteronomy" chapter={34} verse={9} /> developed.

**The LORD's direct charge to Joshua** (1:1-9). The chapter opens at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={1} verse="1-2" />: "Now after the death of Moses the servant of the LORD it came to pass, that the LORD spake unto Joshua the son of Nun, Moses' minister, saying, Moses my servant is dead; now therefore arise, go over this Jordan, thou, and all this people, unto the land which I do give to them, even to the children of Israel." The framework's structural-elements: (a) the temporal pivot ("after the death of Moses") opens the narrative as the post-Mosaic succession-period; (b) the LORD speaks directly to Joshua — the typological framework's first instance of YHWH-to-Joshua direct address in the Joshua corpus; (c) Moses is twice named <LangNote term="eved YHWH" script="עֶבֶד יְהוָה" language="Hebrew" gloss="‘Servant of the LORD.’ Moses' Torah-closing title at Deut 34:5 (drafted Session 20) recurs at Josh 1:1 + 1:2 as the chapter's opening-frame. The framework registers continuity-of-titular-identity across the books: the eved-YHWH framework Moses bore through Deuteronomy carries forward into Joshua's opening narrative. Joshua himself will not bear the title until the end of his own book (Josh 24:29), where the title's reception completes the framework's bookend.">eved YHWH</LangNote> ("the servant of the LORD") at the chapter's opening; (d) the imperative "arise, go over this Jordan" issues the charge.

The framework's land-promise restatement at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={1} verse="3-4" /> recalls the patriarchal framework: "Every place that the sole of your foot shall tread upon, that have I given unto you, as I said unto Moses." The framework echoes <VerseRef book="deuteronomy" chapter={11} verse="24" />'s every-place-treaded promise (drafted Session 16) and the broader Abrahamic land-promise at <VerseRef book="genesis" chapter={15} verse={18} />.

The chapter then installs the load-bearing succession-framework at 1:5-9.

**Joshua's command to the officers and the eastern tribes** (1:10-15). The chapter then installs the practical-preparation framework at 1:10-11: the officers go through the host commanding the people to prepare victuals for the three-day crossing of the Jordan. The three-day timeline marks the framework's immediate-action register.

The framework then addresses the trans-Jordan tribes at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={1} verse="12-15" />: the Reubenites, Gadites, and half-Manasseh — whose inheritance is east of the Jordan per <VerseRef book="numbers" chapter={32} verse="1-42" /> (drafted Session 15) — are reminded of Moses' commission. The framework registers the trans-Jordan tribes' covenantal-solidarity-obligation: the inheritance-rest is not individual-tribal but corporate-covenantal.

**The people's pledge of obedience** (1:16-18). The chapter closes with the framework's covenantal-reciprocity register.

The framework's narrative-completion of the Joshua-succession typology: <VerseRef book="numbers" chapter={27} verse="12-23" /> drafted the formal semikhah + hod-transfer + Yehoshua/Iesous name-typology; <VerseRef book="deuteronomy" chapter={31} verse="1-23" /> drafted the public + LORD-direct commissioning + the chazaq-ve-amets formula's first three installations; <VerseRef book="deuteronomy" chapter={34} verse={9} /> drafted the narrative-completion through Israel's hearkening; the chapter at hand completes the framework with the LORD's direct charge to Joshua + the chazaq-ve-amets formula's full five-fold installation + the people's reciprocal pledge. The framework spans six chapters across three books — Pentateuch's last book and Deuteronomistic History's first book — composing one of the OT's clearest single succession-typology frameworks.
