Joshua 17 is Manasseh's allotment chapter — the second of the two Joseph-tribes allotments (Ephraim having received the prior chapter, Josh 16). Three movements structure it: Manasseh's male-line clans receive their portions, then Zelophehad's daughters receive theirs "according to the commandment of the LORD" (17:1-6); Manasseh's territorial borders are detailed, with cities reaching into Issachar and Asher and the candid territorial-incomplete notation about Canaanites who would dwell in the land (17:7-13); the children of Joseph (Ephraim and Manasseh together) complain about having only one lot, and Joshua responds (17:14-18).

**Manasseh's male-line clans + Zelophehad's daughters** (17:1-6). The chapter opens at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={17} verse={1} />: "There was also a lot for the tribe of Manasseh; for he was the firstborn of Joseph; to wit, for Machir the firstborn of Manasseh, the father of Gilead: because he was a man of war, therefore he had Gilead and Bashan." Machir's east-Jordan inheritance is noted as already given (it was distributed under Moses per Num 32 and Josh 13). The chapter then lists the six remaining male-line clans of Manasseh at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={17} verse={2} /> — Abiezer, Helek, Asriel, Shechem, Hepher, Shemida.

The chapter's structural heart follows at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={17} verse="3-4" />: "But Zelophehad, the son of Hepher, the son of Gilead, the son of Machir, the son of Manasseh, had no sons, but daughters: and these are the names of his daughters, Mahlah, and Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah. And they came near before Eleazar the priest, and before Joshua the son of Nun, and before the princes, saying, The LORD commanded Moses to give us an inheritance among our brethren. Therefore according to the commandment of the LORD he gave them an inheritance among the brethren of their father." This is the territorial-execution of the inheritance-precedent established at <VerseRef book="numbers" chapter={27} verse="1-11" /> (Session 15) and refined at <VerseRef book="numbers" chapter={36} verse="1-12" /> (Session 15). The five daughters' names recur exactly — Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, Tirzah — across all three passages, signaling deliberate narrative-continuity. The chapter at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={17} verse={5} /> notes the arithmetic consequence: "And there fell ten portions to Manasseh" — six for the male-line clans, five for the daughters' lineage (whose territory falls within Manasseh per Num 36's marriage-constraint resolution).

**Manasseh's borders + the territorial-incomplete notation** (17:7-13). Manasseh's coast runs "from Asher to Michmethah, that lieth before Shechem" (17:7), with cross-tribal arrangements: Tappuah on the border belongs to Ephraim (17:8); Ephraim has cities among Manasseh's cities (17:9); Manasseh has cities in Issachar and Asher — Bethshean, Ibleam, Dor, Endor, Taanach, Megiddo (17:11). Then comes the candid notation at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={17} verse="12-13" />: "Yet the children of Manasseh could not drive out the inhabitants of those cities; but the Canaanites would dwell in that land. Yet it came to pass, when the children of Israel were waxen strong, that they put the Canaanites to tribute, but did not utterly drive them out." This is the third territorial-incomplete notation in the allotment block, and it is the most candid of the three — Manasseh explicitly "could not" drive out, the Canaanites "would dwell" by their own will, and tribute was imposed only once Israel grew strong.

**The children of Joseph's complaint** (17:14-18). The chapter closes with a small dialogue scene. The children of Joseph (Ephraim and Manasseh jointly) come to Joshua at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={17} verse={14} />: "Why hast thou given me but one lot and one portion to inherit, seeing I am a great people, forasmuch as the LORD hath blessed me hitherto?" The complaint reads as a request for more territory on the ground of population size and divine blessing. Joshua's answer at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={17} verse={15} /> redirects effort rather than reapportioning land: "If thou be a great people, then get thee up to the wood country, and cut down for thyself there in the land of the Perizzites and of the giants, if mount Ephraim be too narrow for thee." The children of Joseph push back at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={17} verse={16} />: "The hill is not enough for us: and all the Canaanites that dwell in the land of the valley have chariots of iron." Joshua's final word at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={17} verse="17-18" /> reaffirms both promise and effort: "Thou art a great people, and hast great power: thou shalt not have one lot only: But the mountain shall be thine; for it is a wood, and thou shalt cut it down: and the outgoings of it shall be thine: for thou shalt drive out the Canaanites, though they have iron chariots, and though they be strong."

**The Zelophehad-daughters narrative-execution — Num 27 + Num 36 cross-session connection-back.** Joshua 17:3-6 is the territorial-execution of a three-passage narrative arc that began at the Plains of Moab in Numbers and now reaches its land-distribution completion at the cis-Jordan allotment. Two separate Mosaic-instruction frameworks are executed simultaneously here, which makes the scene structurally analogous to Josh 8's three-fold execution of Deuteronomic instruction (drafted Session 22):

(1) **Num 27:1-11 — the inheritance-precedent.** The five daughters of Zelophehad (already named: Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, Tirzah) come to the tabernacle door and ask, "Why should the name of our father be done away from among his family, because he hath no son? Give unto us therefore a possession among the brethren of our father" (Num 27:4). Moses brings the case before the LORD; the LORD answers, "The daughters of Zelophehad speak right: thou shalt surely give them a possession of an inheritance among their father's brethren" (Num 27:7). The case becomes a general statute: if a man die without a son, his inheritance passes to his daughter.

(2) **Num 36:1-12 — the tribal-marriage-constraint refinement.** The chief fathers of Gilead come to Moses with a downstream concern: if the daughters marry outside Manasseh, their inherited land will pass to another tribe at the Jubilee, eroding the Manasseh allotment. The LORD's response refines the Num 27 statute: "Let them marry to whom they think best; only to the family of the tribe of their father shall they marry" (Num 36:6). The daughters comply: "For Mahlah, Tirzah, and Hoglah, and Milcah, and Noah, the daughters of Zelophehad, were married unto their fathers brothers' sons" (Num 36:11). Both the inheritance-right and the tribal-integrity concern are honored simultaneously.

(3) **Josh 17:3-6 — the territorial-execution at the cis-Jordan allotment-stage.** The daughters now come not to the tabernacle door but to Eleazar the priest, Joshua, and the princes — the same governance triad that has been distributing the allotments (cf. Josh 14:1). They cite the Mosaic instruction: "The LORD commanded Moses to give us an inheritance among our brethren." The text records the execution: "Therefore according to the commandment of the LORD he gave them an inheritance among the brethren of their father." The chapter then notes the arithmetic consequence — Manasseh receives ten portions (six male-line clans plus the five-daughters' lineage now counted alongside).

The structural-significance: this is the OT's clearest single instance of a multi-stage covenant-promise-and-execution arc preserved verbatim across three books (Numbers + Joshua). The daughters' names recur exactly in all three passages — a literary marker of deliberate continuity, not coincidence. The Num 27:7 phrase "thou shalt surely give them a possession" issues the promise; the Num 36:6 phrase "only to the family of the tribe of their father shall they marry" refines the conditions; the Josh 17:4 phrase "according to the commandment of the LORD he gave them an inheritance" closes the arc with executed land-grant. The same pattern of multi-stage covenant-promise-and-execution drives the Caleb-promise arc (Num 14:24 → Num 14:30 → Num 32:11-12 → Deut 1:36 → Josh 14:6-15) named in the Josh 14 LangNote drafted prior block. Two such complete OT-internal-promise-and-execution arcs now both close in the Joshua allotment chapters — a structural reflection of the book's role as the literary site where Pentateuchal-promise becomes Pentateuchal-fulfillment.

A further structural detail worth noting: the Num 27 daughters address Moses-at-the-tabernacle-door; the Josh 17 daughters address Joshua-with-Eleazar-and-the-princes. The succession-of-leadership from Moses to Joshua is enacted in the very scene where the daughters return to claim the promise made under Moses — and Eleazar, the same high priest who was present alongside Moses in Num 27, is still present alongside Joshua in Josh 17. The continuity of governance is what makes the continuity of promise-execution possible.
