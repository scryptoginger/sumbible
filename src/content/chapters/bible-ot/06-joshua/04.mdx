Joshua 4 closes the two-chapter Jordan-crossing unit. The chapter has four major movements: the LORD's command for the twelve stones and twelve men, one from each tribe (4:1-7); the stones taken up + the parallel set-of-twelve in the riverbed + crossing complete + the LORD magnifies Joshua (4:8-14); the priests come up out of Jordan + the waters return (4:15-18); the stones pitched at Gilgal + the intergenerational-memory pedagogy + the explicit Jordan-Red-Sea parallel (4:19-24).

**The twelve stones command** (4:1-7). The chapter opens at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={4} verse="2-3" /> with the LORD's command to Joshua: "Take you twelve men out of the people, out of every tribe a man, And command ye them, saying, Take you hence out of the midst of Jordan, out of the place where the priests' feet stood firm, twelve stones." **The two-set-of-twelve-stones framework + the intergenerational-memory pedagogy.** The chapter's framework at 4:8 (the twelve stones carried to Gilgal) + 4:9 (the twelve stones set up in the riverbed) installs a structurally-distinctive two-set-of-twelve-stones framework. Standard commentary across multiple traditions reads the two-set structure variously.

(1) **Two-source readings.** Some readings (the source-critical tradition) register the two sets of stones as evidence of two narrative-sources in the chapter's composition — one source registering the Gilgal stones, another registering the riverbed stones.

(2) **Two-register readings.** Other readings register the two-set structure as theologically-substantive: the accessible-public Gilgal memorial + the hidden-but-testified riverbed marker operate at two distinct pedagogy-registers. The Gilgal stones function at the public-catechetical-pedagogy register (the framework Josh 4:21 invokes — "when your children shall ask"); the riverbed stones function at the hidden-but-perpetually-witnessing register (the framework's "they are there unto this day" at Josh 4:9 — the stones are unseen but the framework registers their continuing presence as ongoing testimony).
