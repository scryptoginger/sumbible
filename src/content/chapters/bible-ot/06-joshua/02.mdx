Joshua 2 narrates the spies' mission to Jericho and Rahab's protection of them. The chapter has four major movements: the spies sent + Rahab hides them on her rooftop under flax-stalks (2:1-7); Rahab's confession + the covenant oath (2:8-14); the scarlet-cord token + the spies' departure through the window (2:15-21); the spies' three-day mountain hiding + their report to Joshua (2:22-24). The chapter's theological center is Rahab's confession at 2:9-11. The chapter has substantial NT reception across three distinct doctrinal registers.

**The spies sent and Rahab's protection** (2:1-7). The chapter opens at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={2} verse={1} />: "And Joshua the son of Nun sent out of Shittim two men to spy secretly, saying, Go view the land, even Jericho. **Rahab's three-fold NT reception across distinct doctrinal registers.** The chapter at hand is one of a handful of OT chapters whose central figure is cited at three or more distinct NT doctrinal registers. Rahab's three NT citations together compose one of the OT-NT canon's clearest single multi-register receptions of an OT figure.

(1) **Hebrews 11:31 — faith-paradigm register.** <VerseRef book="hebrews" chapter={11} verse={31} /> reads Rahab at the faith-paradigm-of-Hebrews-11 cloud-of-witnesses framework: "By faith the harlot Rahab perished not with them that believed not, when she had received the spies with peace." The framework pairs Rahab with the faith-paradigm at <VerseRef book="hebrews" chapter={11} verse={30} /> (Jericho's walls fell by faith — the chapter at hand's narrative-counterpart at Josh 6). Standard NT commentary reads the two verses as the Hebrews-author's deliberate paired-faith-paradigm reading of Josh 2 and Josh 6 together: the conquest's faith-paradigm + the Canaanite's faith-paradigm composed as paired-instances of the same framework. The chapter at hand is read at Heb 11:31 as the faith-paradigm-precedes-the-conquest-narrative register; Josh 6 is read at Heb 11:30 as the faith-paradigm-enacts-the-conquest-narrative register.

(2) **James 2:25 — faith-and-works-justification register.** <VerseRef book="james" chapter={2} verse={25} /> reads Rahab at the faith-and-works-justification framework: "Likewise also was not Rahab the harlot justified by works, when she had received the messengers, and had sent them out another way?" The framework pairs Rahab with Abraham (<VerseRef book="james" chapter={2} verse="21-24" />) as the two cited paradigms within the broader argument that "faith without works is dead" (Jas 2:17, 26). The structural-significance of the pairing: Abraham at the patriarchal-foundational register + Rahab at the Canaanite-outsider register — the framework cites two OT figures across radically-different social-locations as the same faith-and-works framework's paradigms.

(3) **Matthew 1:5 — messianic-genealogy register.** <VerseRef book="matthew" chapter={1} verse={5} /> includes Rahab in the messianic genealogy of Christ: "And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab." Rahab is one of four women named in the Matthean genealogy (Tamar at 1:3, Rahab at 1:5, Ruth at 1:5, and "her that had been the wife of Urias" — Bathsheba — at 1:6), all with irregular-by-conventional-measure backgrounds. Standard commentary across multiple traditions reads the four-women framework as Matthew's deliberate inclusion of figures with irregular-conventional-status within the messianic line: the framework registers the messianic line as embracing what the regular framework would have excluded. Rahab as Boaz's mother places her at the threshold of the Ruth narrative and four generations before David.

The three-register reception's structural-significance: Rahab's framework operates across three doctrinal registers (faith-as-trust-and-reception; faith-as-active-with-works; faith-as-included-in-messianic-line). The three citations are not coincidental but compose a coherent NT-reception framework: Rahab as the OT-paradigm of the marginal-Canaanite-outsider whom the LORD includes by faith, whose faith is structurally-active in protective-works, and whose family-line is embraced within the messianic genealogy.

**Rahab's confession and the Deut 4:39 framework.** The chapter at hand's framework at <VerseRef book="joshua" chapter={2} verse={11} /> — "the LORD your God, he is God in heaven above, and in earth beneath" — installs the framework that <VerseRef book="deuteronomy" chapter={4} verse={39} /> (drafted Session 16) had stated in Israel's voice: "Know therefore this day, and consider it in thine heart, that the LORD he is God in heaven above, and upon the earth beneath: there is none else." The framework registers as Rahab — the Canaanite-outsider, the marginal social-location — speaking the same monotheistic declaration the Mosaic-generation Israelites had been given as the covenantal-confession.

The framework's structural-significance: the conquest's narrative-opening at the chapter at hand installs a Canaanite-confession-of-YHWH preceding the conquest's military-execution. The framework operates as the narrative's structural-paradox: the conquest, in its military-juridical register, operates against the Canaanite peoples corporately; the conquest, in its faith-paradigm register at the chapter at hand, includes the Canaanite-individual who confesses YHWH. Standard commentary across multiple traditions reads the paradox along distinct registers — some register it as evidence the conquest's framework was never strictly ethnic but covenantal-juridical (those who confess YHWH are included); some register it as evidence the herem framework's hyperbolic-rhetorical register (Session 17-19's spectrum); some register it as evidence the framework's structural-tension with the broader OT-NT trajectory toward Gentile-inclusion. SumBible reports the framework's narrative-juridical register at the chapter at hand; the broader herem-and-inclusion theological-interpretive question operates at multiple commentary-traditions' distinct depths.

The framework's NT trajectory at <VerseRef book="acts" chapter={10} verse={34} /> (Peter at Cornelius' house: "Of a truth I perceive that God is no respecter of persons") and at <VerseRef book="galatians" chapter={3} verse={28} /> ("There is neither Jew nor Greek... for ye are all one in Christ Jesus") develops the framework's inclusion-register into the broader NT-Gentile-inclusion framework. The chapter at hand operates as the OT-narrative's earliest single instance of the framework's positive-inclusion register applied to a Canaanite individual.
